What broke

Three things the analysis had been treating as established. The question round went and checked them.

"Four classes are built but unreleased: codex, context-engineering, prompt-engineering, for-business."
All four are shipped. Codex has 47 videos and was updated on 2026-07-12. Prompt-engineering has 13, context-engineering 11, for-business 7. Paying members describe watching them.
This "verified ground truth" was inferred from the classes' absence from traffic — but absence of traffic is a discovery problem, not a shipping problem. There are 78 videos of built product that nobody can find. That reframes an entire branch of the plan.
"The recovery-email lever is worth $13-27k per 60 days."
It's worth roughly $3-8k. The estimate assumed all 1,510 abandoners were reachable. Only 240 of them left an email address.
The lever survives — abandoners really don't come back on their own (0.6% organic recovery) — but it drops below the mobile-checkout fix, and its own prerequisite is capturing email earlier.
"refund_processed carries no amount, so refunds can't be netted out."
The property is amountRefunded, populated on 35 of 35 events. The pack looked for amount. Refunds are computable: 2.46% by count, 4.37% by USD ($7,277 over 180d).
Refunds are half what was feared and are simply not a problem for this business — but no revenue figure in the earlier analysis nets them out.

The fork it fed
If lift is holding or growing, videos remain the growth engine. If it's decaying, the audience is saturating — shift effort from top-of-funnel video to mid-funnel conversion.

Actual

And "make more videos" only holds inside the Q3 topic cluster — the Anki video, wrong topic, produced negative lift (−$1,393).

Q5
How long do buyers deliberate?
prior wrong›
The fork it fed
If most buy more than 24h after first touch, this is a nurture business — invest in the consideration path and stop over-reading launch pulses. If most buy same-session, it's impulse — time launches for pulses.
Prior vs actual
Prior guess
Median 2-4 days; ~55% of purchases happen more than 24h after first touch
Actual
Median ~4 minutes. 82-89% buy within 30 minutes. Only 6-9% take over 24h.
Answer
This is an impulse business, decisively. The answerer stress-tested its own result against the person-merging bias: 39% of buyers have no client-side history, but that bias pushes measured lag down, so it recomputed on the 731 buyers who do have history — and the conclusion held (median 0.07h, 82% within 30 min). The finding survives because the bias runs against it.
Verdict
Impulse-driven. The publish-day revenue spikes are real signal, not noise to discount. Do not invest in a long post-visit nurture path.
Caveats
This proves buyers convert in-session, not that they never deliberated. The real consideration happens on YouTube, off-site, where PostHog cannot see it.
Q6
Does the newsletter path convert? — the answer nobody asked for is the good one
reframed email›
The fork it fed
If subscribers convert multiples higher but the list is small, list-growth on-ramps are the ignored lever. If the list is healthy, email execution is the lever. If it barely converts, drop email strategically.

Prior guess

Actual

And it's mailed 2.5 times a month with zero conversion or recovery emails.

Q11
Does retention predict revenue better than format?
prior inverted›
The fork it fed
If retention predicts better, optimize for retention and hook when picking topics. If the format label wins, keep using the workflow-vs-news split as the cue.
Prior vs actual
Prior guess
Retention predicts revenue-per-view better than the coarse format label
Actual
Retention doesn't predict at all — its correlation is negative (ρ −0.30, p=0.25). The format label scores ρ +0.66 (p=0.007).
Answer
Retention and revenue pull in opposite directions. News videos actually retain better (30.8% vs 26.8%) while earning 4-9x less per 1k views. Retention adds nothing once format is known (within-format ρ +0.04 / −0.03). And views correlate negatively with revenue-per-1k (ρ −0.65).
Verdict
Pick topics by format; treat retention as a craft target, never as the selector. The mechanism is audience intent, not attention: viral news hits pull large, well-retained, low-intent crowds. A workflow video selects for people with a job to do, and those people buy.

CTR could not be tested — VidTempla rejects impressions/CTR metrics. That half is genuinely unanswered, not answered-and-null.
n=17. The format correlation clears significance; the retention one does not — the honest statement is "retention shows no detectable relationship", not "retention is proven to hurt".

Cross-cutting: one bug behind three symptoms

The identity-stitching bug

Server-side purchase events aren't stitched to the anonymous browsing person. That single defect explains 86% of purchases missing utm_source, 39% of buyers with no client history, and part of the recovery-sizing fog. Fix it and every attribution number in this report sharpens.

The business became launch-shaped

Between-launch baseline revenue fell 75-80%. Video lift is growing; everything that isn't a video has stopped paying. That's why the mid-funnel work is urgent rather than optional — right now, revenue happens on publish days.

78 videos nobody can find

Four classes — codex (47 videos), prompt-engineering (13), context-engineering (11), for-business (7) — are shipped, updated, and drawing near-zero traffic. This is the largest piece of already-paid-for work sitting idle in the business. It needs discovery, not production.

Instrumentation debt is the tax on every answer

PostHog sees 11% of the email list. Refunds don't link to purchases (13 of 35). Discount flags didn't exist before June. Bot flags aren't populated. Each of these turned a clean question into a bounded estimate — and each is a small fix.

What the sketch round had already established

Before the questions, five builders were each blinded to any prior analysis and derived their own findings from the raw data. Independent agreement between blinded builders is the verification. These held up — and the question round then re-sized two of them.

5 / 5
builders
Checkout is the binding constraint, not traffic. 1,613 abandons in 60d against 17 recovered; ~$131k of expressed intent vs $44k realized. Q1 and Q8 later re-sized this: the recovery half is ~$3-8k, and the mobile half is ~$9k.
5 / 5
builders
Attribution is half-blind; YouTube's true share is ~62%, not the measured 34%. Every per-video ROI figure undercounts by ~2x. Q2 and the cross-cutting bug later explained the mechanism.
4 / 5
builders
Workflow videos out-earn news ~10-19x per view. Q11 confirmed and sharpened this to 4.3x on means / 8.9x on medians with full view data — and killed retention as an alternative predictor. Q3 then found the deeper cut: it's the operating-method topic, not the format label.
3 / 5
builders
No revenue crisis — April was a promo artifact; baseline ~$23-26k/month. Q10 partly overturned this: April was a volume event, not a discount event, and there IS an unresolved 57% post-April trough.

How this was produced

  1. Blinded sketch round. Five builders each got the raw data pack with the orchestrator's conclusions deliberately withheld — the pack states what is, never what it means. Each derived its own findings and built a working surface of a different underlying model. Agreement between builders who couldn't see each other is evidence; agreement with a shared conclusion would have been an echo.
  2. Question round. A questioner agent — given the business, the sources, and the converged findings as priors to stress, not settled facts — generated 16 candidate questions, several deliberately designed to break a finding. Every question carried a fork ("if X do A, if Y do B") and a prior guess, so surprise could be measured rather than asserted.
  3. Four answerers, one per source, ran the real queries in parallel: PostHog (person-level counterfactuals; revenue attribution), VidTempla + the ACS platform (comments, retention, class state), Bento (email infrastructure). They were forbidden to approximate — "not answerable" was a valid answer, and a fabricated number would poison the decision.
  4. The connectors died mid-run. PostHog, VidTempla, and the ACS platform dropped for about an hour. Every blocked agent held its position with pre-drafted queries rather than guessing, and reported the blocker. The round resumed on reconnect. No number in this report was estimated to fill a gap.

Still open

Named honestly, because a report that hides its gaps is a confident lie.

The 57% post-April revenue trough. Real, sustained 11 weeks, and confounded with a product-lineup change. It deserves its own investigation and is the single biggest unexplained thing in the data.
Whether the four classes would sell if anyone could find them. Comment demand is intent, not revenue — and the one Codex video in the revenue set monetized 4th-worst, which is a genuine warning that Codex enthusiasm may be chatter rather than buying intent.
What the untrafficked /watch free-lesson pages are worth. No exposure means no data. Q2 says a warmer on-ramp is the YouTube lever; the only way to price it is to run traffic through it.
Why abandoners abandon. Events show where people drop, never the motive. Q8 localizes it to the Stripe payment page on mobile, which is the closest thing to a cause this data can produce.
True willingness-to-pay at $397. Realized price is ~40% below list every month. That's a pricing question a live test answers and backward-looking data cannot.
