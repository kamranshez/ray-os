### What Worked This Month

**1. **4. The lifetime plan deadline created $12,406 over Apr 21-22 without a new video.** Two days of pre-expiry buying ($5,190 + $7,216) outperformed the entire 4-day Apr 9-12 cluster ($6,065 from 3 videos). Deadline urgency is the single highest-leverage revenue lever available in a given month.

### What Didn't

**1. Three uploads in 36 hours (Apr 9-10) repeated the March 19-21 stacking failure.** Combined Apr 9-12 unique-day revenue: $6,065. Single video ASAaKhK1B5w (Apr 15) did $13,414 in its own 3-day window. Stacking 3 videos did half the revenue of 1 well-positioned video.

The Apr 9 cluster may have cannibalized reach across all three videos. V5 (24.3K on its own window 2 weeks later) shows the channel can still get views -- just not at the cadence that made March work.

### Methodology and its limits

April's attribution coverage is **25.1%** (91 tagged purchases out of 363 total). This is the "Good" confidence range (20-60%) per the methodology rubric, and a major improvement from March's 8%. H7 from March (fix checkout UTM persistence) appears to have partially shipped.

At 25.1% coverage, tagged revenue ($21,167.59) is still a lower bound but is now a meaningful directional signal. Per-video tagged purchases (with samples of 3-31 per video) are large enough to rank videos by effectiveness, though still noisy for exact conversion rate math.

The 3-day Stripe window methodology is approximated here using PostHog daily purchase totals. PostHog daily totals count all `purchase_complete` server events regardless of UTM -- they are the closest available proxy to Stripe daily charge counts while the script is unavailable.

One complicating factor: the April 21-22 pricing deadline (lifetime plan expiry) inflated 5 days of "baseline" revenue to near video-window levels ($4,043/day avg). The baseline calculation below reports two versions -- all non-window days and a narrower "organic baseline" excluding the deadline window.

**Recommended fix still needed:** Get to 80%+ tagged coverage by verifying the checkout UTM pass-through is working for subscription purchases, not just one-time checkouts. Coverage at 25.1% suggests one-time checkouts are now tagged but subscription renewals (the other ~75%) are still dark.

## Part 4: Hypotheses to Test

### Core Tensions from April

**1. Revenue per video is up 139%, views per video are down 51%.** The pricing change is dominating the revenue signal. It is now very hard to know whether pitch improvements or cadence changes drove better revenue -- the AOV increase alone explains most of the lift. May needs a controlled comparison: same pricing, different pitch tactics, to isolate what is actually moving conversion.

**2. The V4 and V5 gap is the most useful comparison in the dataset.** V4 (10.9K views, 2.80% CTR, $1,230/1K RPM) and V5 (24.3K views, 1.08% CTR, $1,022/1K RPM) both did outstanding work. V4 did more revenue per view on a smaller audience. This suggests the "3 levels" framework + social-proof title combination is extremely high-value and should be tested repeatedly.

**3. The Apr 9-10 cluster repeated March 19-21 and failed the same way.** Three videos in 36 hours produced $6,065 combined. The Apr 15 solo video produced $13,414. Ray has now run this experiment twice and gotten the same result both times. The cadence question is settled for regular uploads: one upload per 5-7 days outperforms 3 uploads in 2 days.

**4. The pricing deadline generated more revenue per day than any video-window in April except V5.** This is a new, untested lever. If Ray can run deadline campaigns quarterly (or per product cycle), each one represents a $10K-$15K 2-day revenue event with no upload required.

---

### H1 (carried from Feb/Mar): Masterclass-first opening with urgency
**Status: Still open, not cleanly testable this month.** No transcripts available to verify placement. V4 and V5 both performed strongly and both have "Level up with my Agent Engineer workshop" as the first description link. Whether the in-video CTA lands in the first 90 seconds is unknown without audio. Priority remains high.

### H2 (from March): Retire the BIRTHDAY coupon earlier
**Status: VALIDATED.** April AOV avg $232 vs March's BIRTHDAY-depressed range of $109-$156. Removing the coupon raised AOV by 49-113% depending on which March window you compare. This is the clearest single-month validation of any hypothesis in the audit history. The revenue lift from AOV increase ($81K April vs $63K March) more than offset the 18% decline in purchase count.

### H3 (from March): One upload per week, not three
**Status: PARTIALLY VALIDATED.** Five uploads in April (down from 9 in March). But the Apr 9-10 cluster still stacked 3 videos in 36 hours and underperformed. The correct framing: it is not just "5 uploads" -- it is "no more than 1 upload per 5-7 days, without exception." April violated this for the first cluster. Test in May: strict 5-7 day spacing across all uploads.

### H4 (from March): Personal discovery opening is the new working hook
**Status: Cannot verify.** No transcripts. Chapter markers show "Introduction" as first chapter on V4 and V5 but content is unknown. Carry to May with transcript capture requirement.

### H5 (from March): Always pitch with urgency on 50K+ view videos
**Status: Not tested.** No April video crossed 50K views. Carry forward.

### H6 (from March): Retire "Anthropic Just Dropped the Feature Nobody Knew They Needed" title
**Status: VALIDATED.** No repeated titles in April. No direct title reuse within the month.

### H7 (from March): Fix checkout UTM persistence
**Status: PARTIALLY VALIDATED.** Coverage went from 8% (March) to 25.1% (April). One-time checkout UTMs are now being preserved for roughly 1-in-4 purchases. Subscription renewals are still dark (~75% of transactions). Next step: verify if subscription renewal webhooks can also pass the original checkout UTM, or if a first-purchase attribution model is sufficient.

### H8 (new): Pricing deadline campaigns generate comparable per-day revenue to video launches without production cost
April Apr 21-22 generated $12,406 in 2 days ($6,203/day) with no new upload. V4 generated $6,112 day-1. The deadline days are actually in the same revenue tier as a well-pitched video launch. **Test:** run a deliberate price increase with a 7-day countdown, measure total deadline-window revenue vs the closest video-window revenue in the same month.

### H9 (new): "Agent Engineer workshop" rebranding changes conversion rate vs "Claude Code Masterclass"
All April descriptions use the new framing. No A/B control this month. **Test:** May mid-month A/B -- one video description uses "Agent Engineer workshop" with current URL, another uses "Claude Code Masterclass" with the same URL. Compare CTR on the description link (PostHog pageviews filtered by video ID).

### H10 (new): Workflow tutorial format with 3+ numbered levels outperforms feature-announce format per view
V4 data supports this strongly (2.80% CTR vs 0.26-1.85% for feature-announces). **Test:** two workflow tutorials in May vs two feature-announces, compare CTR and tagged RPM.
