There is one chart that explains why this entire class exists. And almost everyone who shares it reads it wrong.

It comes from METR, a research group that measures how capable AI is actually getting. And their method is the cleanest one I have seen.

Source: https://metr.org/

---

## How the chart works

You take a task. You measure how long it takes a skilled human to finish it. Then you find the longest task an AI can complete on its own.

That length is the score.

Plot it over time and you get a line going straight up. We went from two-second tasks back in 2019 to most of a workday today. The top models are now landing somewhere around twelve to sixteen hours.

## The part everyone gets wrong

Twelve hours does not mean the agent grinds away for twelve hours.

It means a human would have taken twelve hours to do that task. The agent does the same work much faster. Often several times faster.

And there is a catch hiding in the number. That twelve to sixteen hours is measured at fifty percent success. So it is a coin flip at the top end. Not something you hand off and walk away from yet.

So read it like this. A human would need twelve hours. The agent does it quicker, and gets it right about half the time.

## Now here is what nobody points out

That chart is measuring one agent. One task. One context window.

On the map from this class, that is Level one. The bottom rung. The whole headline trend that the entire industry is losing its mind over is happening at the very bottom of our ladder.

## You do not have to wait for the doubling

That is the whole reason loops exist.

You have two options. Sit and wait seven months for the model to get twice as good by itself. Or climb.

Because a worker loop, a discovery loop, a fleet running while you sleep, that is how you take a twelve-hour time horizon and stack it into a week of work. Out of today's model. Tonight.

Every level you go up multiplies the length of work you can hand off. You are not waiting on the model. You are wrapping it.

## Demo

This one is a screen walkthrough, not a terminal session. Here is what the camera shows.

1. Pull up the METR time horizon chart live. Trace the curve from the two-second tasks in 2019 up to the twelve to sixteen hour mark today.
2. Freeze on the top point. Drop a single label on the line: this is one agent, one task, one window.
3. Overlay the word "L1" directly on that point.
4. Cut to the eight-level loop stack from the intro. Slot the entire METR curve into the bottom box. Hold it there so the size difference lands. The whole famous chart is rung one.
5. Pan up the stack. As you pass L4 and L5, show the twelve-hour block multiplying into days, then a week.
6. End on the full tower with the chart tiny at the base.

The point of the demo is the size difference. The thing the internet is amazed by is the smallest box in the picture.

> The chart everyone is sharing measures one agent doing one task. That is rung one of eight. Loops are how you climb the other seven without waiting for the model.
