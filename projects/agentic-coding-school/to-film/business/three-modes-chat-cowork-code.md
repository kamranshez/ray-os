## The Three Modes: Chat, Cowork, and Code

Most business users only experience Chat mode — and never discover the mode that actually changes everything.

> "Chat is the conversational interface. I talk to Claude the way I'd talk to a fast, knowledgeable associate sitting across the table... Cowork is the autonomous mode, and it's the one that changes everything. I point Claude at a folder on my computer, give it a task, and it goes and does it. It reads files, creates new ones, edits existing documents, and makes its own decisions about how to get from A to B."
> — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)

### What to Cover

1. **Chat** — brainstorming strategy, analyzing a document, getting a quick opinion on a deal, drafting from scratch. This is what 99% of people think AI is.
2. **Cowork** — the autonomous mode. Hand it a folder of invoices, a stack of client briefs, a set of closing documents. It reads, processes, creates, and edits without you micromanaging each step. Demo: point Cowork at a folder of raw files and let it organize, summarize, and produce deliverables.
3. **Code** — building custom tools for your specific workflow. The lawyer built an audio conversion tool for contracts because of an accessibility need.

> "Most lawyers who have used ChatGPT or similar tools have only experienced this mode."
> — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)

### Key Demo

Show the same task (e.g. reviewing a stack of client documents) done in Chat mode vs Cowork mode. The contrast should be visceral — one is a back-and-forth conversation, the other is "go do this" and come back to finished work product.

### The Insight

> "A team of three associates at a mid-size firm would have needed until morning to produce that analysis. I had the core of it in under two hours."
> — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)
