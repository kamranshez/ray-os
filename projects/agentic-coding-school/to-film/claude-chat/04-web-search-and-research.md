## Video 4 — Web Search & Deep Research

**Goal**: Viewer understands the difference between web search and deep research, and knows when to use each for real-time information.

---

### HOOK (0:00–0:30)

> "One of the biggest complaints about AI is that it makes stuff up or gives you outdated information. Claude fixes this with two features: web search for quick lookups, and deep research for thorough reports. In this video, I'll show you both — and when to use which."

---

### SECTION 1: The Problem — Stale Information (0:30–2:00)

**On screen**: Claude giving an answer, then showing it's from training data.

**Demo the problem**:
Type (with web search OFF):
```
What are the latest features Claude released this month?
```
- Show Claude hedging or giving outdated info
- > "Without web search, Claude is working from training data — which could be weeks or months old. For anything time-sensitive, that's a problem."

Now toggle web search ON and ask the same question:
- Show Claude actually searching the web
- Show current, accurate results
- > "Night and day. Now it's pulling real-time information."

---

### SECTION 2: Web Search — Quick Real-Time Lookups (2:00–5:00)

**On screen**: Web search toggle highlighted.

> "Web search is the basic version. It's fast — Claude searches the web, reads a few pages, and gives you an answer with sources."

**How to enable it**:
- Click the "+" or settings on the chat input
- Toggle "Web search" on
- It's often on by default

**Demo 1: Current events**
```
What happened in tech news today?
```
- Show results with sources cited

**Demo 2: Price check**
```
What's the current price of a MacBook Air M4?
```
- Show real-time pricing pulled from the web

**Demo 3: Fact verification**
```
Is it true that Anthropic raised a new funding round recently? 
Give me the details with sources.
```
- Show sourced answer

> "Web search is perfect for quick lookups where you need current facts. It takes a few seconds, not minutes."

---

### SECTION 3: Deep Research — Thorough Reports (5:00–10:00)

**On screen**: Research mode toggle.

> "Deep research is a different beast entirely. Instead of a quick search, Claude spends 5 to 20 minutes doing real research — reading dozens of pages, cross-referencing information, and compiling a structured report."

**How to enable it**:
- Click the "+" on chat input
- Select "Research" (instead of regular web search)
- Type your research question

**Demo: Competitive research**

Type:
```
Research the top 5 AI note-taking tools (Otter, Fireflies, Granola, Fathom, tl;dv).
For each one: pricing, key features, what users love, what users complain about.
Give me a recommendation for a solo consultant who does 10 client meetings per week.
```

- Show Claude entering research mode
- Show the progress indicator (it takes time)
- > "Notice it says 'Researching...' — it's actually visiting multiple websites, reading reviews, checking pricing pages. This isn't a quick lookup. It's doing real analysis."
- Come back to the completed report
- Show the structured output with sources

> "That report would have taken me an hour to compile manually. Claude did it in 8 minutes with cited sources."

**Reference from competitors**: AI Edge: "I use Claude's research a lot alongside Perplexity and GPT Pro. Claude's research really holds its own." Mentions it can "take 5, 10, 15, 20 minutes."

**Demo: Market research**

Type:
```
Research the online course market for AI tools in 2026.
I want to know: market size, growth trends, pricing ranges, popular platforms,
and what topics have the most demand vs the most competition.
Cite your sources.
```

- Show research running
- Show the final report

> "Market size, growth rates, platform comparisons, topic demand — all sourced. This is the kind of report you'd pay a research assistant for."

---

### SECTION 4: When to Use Which (10:00–11:30)

**On screen**: Comparison table.

| | Web Search | Deep Research |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Seconds | 5–20 minutes |
| Depth | Quick facts, 2–3 sources | Thorough analysis, dozens of sources |
| Best for | Current events, prices, facts | Market research, competitive analysis, reports |
| Credits | Low | Higher (more processing) |
| When to use | "What's the latest..." | "Research and compare..." |

> "Use web search when you need a quick fact. Use deep research when you need a thorough analysis."

**The hybrid approach**:
> "Sometimes I'll start with web search to get a quick overview, then use deep research to go deeper on the most interesting finding. Don't jump straight to deep research for a simple question — it's overkill."

---

### SECTION 5: Tips for Better Research Prompts (11:30–13:00)

1. **Be specific about what you want** — "Research AI writing tools" is vague. "Compare the top 5 AI writing tools by pricing, features, and user reviews" is actionable.
2. **Ask for structure** — "Give me a comparison table" or "Organize by category"
3. **Request sources** — "Cite your sources" ensures you can verify
4. **Set scope** — "Focus on tools under $50/month" or "Only look at options available in the US"
5. **Combine with artifacts** — "Create an interactive comparison dashboard" (covered in artifacts video)
