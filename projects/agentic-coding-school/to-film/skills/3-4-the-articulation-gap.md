## The Articulation Gap

The gap between human expertise and AI expertise isn't the model. It's the fact that nobody has written their expertise down.

### The Problem

Daniel Miessler's core thesis: inside every company there are people like "Cliff" — the 62-year-old who everyone calls when things go down. Cliff has never written anything down. He's never created a skill. He's never done a debrief interview to capture his knowledge. When Cliff retires, that expertise dies.

This is the articulation gap. Companies think AI "can't do what we do." The real problem is they haven't articulated what they do. The expertise exists — it's just trapped in brains, passed from person to person through onboarding, hallway conversations, and complaints. It decays every time someone leaves.

"The way that AI has gotten so good in the last year is largely because of Claude Code. What did Claude Code do? It built context and context management — relations between documents — allowing the model to have knowledge, understanding, and intelligence." — Miessler

Skills are the same thing for any domain. They're expertise packaged as context that a smart model can use.

### How This Differs from 3.3

3.3 "Encoding Your Expertise" = encoding YOUR OWN expertise into a skill.
3.4 "The Articulation Gap" = extracting expertise from OTHER PEOPLE and turning it into skills.

This is the difference between a solopreneur encoding their own workflow and a consultant going into a company and capturing institutional knowledge before it walks out the door.

### What to Show

**The Knowledge Capture Interview skill:**
- Build a skill that interviews a domain expert (or simulates the interview with a role-play)
- The skill asks structured questions: "Walk me through how you do X from start to finish. What do you check first? What are the red flags? What do you do when Y happens? What does everyone get wrong?"
- It captures the answers into a structured skill skeleton: process steps, decision points, edge cases, common mistakes, quality criteria
- Show the output: a draft skill.md ready to refine

**Live demo options:**
1. Interview yourself about something you know well but haven't documented — show how much gets captured
2. Role-play: have Claude play the domain expert (give it a persona prompt) and show the interview skill extracting structured knowledge
3. Take an existing messy document (a wiki page, a Notion doc, a Slack thread) and use the skill to restructure it into a proper skill

**The "Cliff" exercise:**
- "Think of the person at your company that everyone calls when things break. The person who just knows. Now imagine they're retiring in 30 days. This skill is how you capture what's in their head before it's gone."

### Key Quotes (Miessler)

> "All the stuff that is in Robbie's mind and Suz's mind and Chris's mind — it's not written down. Their expertise is not captured. It's passed from human brain to human brain, and they go on vacation, they retire, they jump companies every couple of years. That knowledge just dies."

> "The expertise gap between humans and AI is actually the failure so far of us to articulate all the different chaos things... all those random pieces of knowledge inside of people's brains."

> "Skills are literally expertise packaged for this very smart model to then be able to use."

### Key Insight

The articulation gap is the real bottleneck — not model capability. Every undocumented process is a skill waiting to be written. The person who can walk into a company and extract this knowledge into skills is providing $15K+ of value per engagement (connects to Ch 7.2).
