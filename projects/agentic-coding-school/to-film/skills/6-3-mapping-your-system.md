## Mapping Your System

We've been building skills for the last 20-something lessons. Content skills, marketing skills, operations skills, quality control. They chain together. They run on schedules. They improve themselves.

But when you step back, it can be hard to see the whole picture. How many skills do you have? How do they connect? What's scheduled? What gaps remain?

In this video, we're going to visualize the entire system. One interactive dashboard that maps everything we've built.

### Building the Visualizer (0:00–2:00)

I need a skill that reads my skills directory, understands how they chain and stack, checks what's scheduled, and produces a visual map.

> [SCREEN: Claude terminal]

> [TYPE: /skill-creator]

> [TYPE: "Build a workflow visualizer skill. It should scan all my installed skills, identify how they chain and reference each other, check my scheduled tasks, list my connected apps, and produce an interactive HTML dashboard that maps the entire system."]

Skill Creator builds it. The skill reads from three sources — the skills folder structure, the scheduled tasks config, and the connectors panel. Then it generates an HTML page.

> [SHOW: the skill being built]

Now I run it.

> [TYPE: "Map my entire AI operating system"]

> [SHOW: Claude scanning skills, reading configs, building the dashboard]

### The Dashboard (2:00–5:00)

> [SCREEN: browser — the generated HTML dashboard]

And there it is. My entire AI operating system on one page.

At the top — a master flow showing the major phases. Daily triggers: morning briefing, email check. Weekly triggers: receipt scanner, content planning. On-demand: contract review, proposal generator, research.

> [SHOW: clicking through phases on the dashboard]

I can click on each phase and see the skills inside it. The content phase shows three skills — ideation, scriptwriting, calendar — with arrows showing how they chain. The operations phase shows the receipt scanner feeding into the expense categorizer.

Below that — every skill listed with its status. Active, scheduled, or on-demand. How many times it's been used this month. When it last ran. What connectors it uses.

> [SHOW: the connectors section]

And then the connected apps. Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack. Which skills use which connectors. If a connector goes down, I can see immediately which skills are affected.

> [SHOW: scrolling through the full dashboard]

This is the system we've built across this class. A morning briefing employee. A content director. An operations manager. A marketing director. Quality control with evals, A/B testing, and self-improvement. All chained, stacked, and scheduled.

### The Numbers (5:00–6:00)

And now the payoff question. What does this system replace?

A human content director — research, scripting, calendar management — that's $3,000 to $5,000 a month. An operations person handling receipts, invoices, expense tracking — another $2,000 to $3,000. A marketing coordinator running LinkedIn, drafting emails — another $2,500. A part-time assistant doing the morning briefing work — scanning email, organizing the day — another $1,500.

Total: easily $10,000 to $15,000 a month for a team of four. $120,000 to $180,000 a year.

This system runs on a Claude subscription. $100 a month. $1,200 a year.

Now — and I say this every time — these skills don't replace senior strategic thinking. They don't replace relationship-building. They don't replace the creative work that only you can do. What they replace is the execution layer. The operational labor. The stuff that takes time but doesn't take genius.

And that's the point. You stop being the person who does everything and start being the person who directs a system that does it.

### Why the Map Matters (6:00–7:00)

The visualization isn't just for you. It's a communication tool.

When you show this dashboard to a potential client — and we'll talk about selling systems in Chapter 7 — they immediately understand what they're getting. Not an abstract "AI consulting" pitch. A visual system with named skills, clear workflows, scheduled automation, and connected apps.

And for yourself, it's a planning tool. You look at the map and see gaps. "I've got content creation but no content distribution." "I've got invoicing but no payment follow-up tracking." The auto-improvement skill from Chapter 5 does this programmatically. The dashboard does it visually.

Either way, you know what to build next.
