## Encoding Your Expertise Into Skills

The gap between "review this document" and genuinely useful AI output isn't the model — it's the instructions.

> "The difference between a firm playbook and an individual lawyer's encoded judgment is the difference between giving someone a recipe and teaching them how to cook."
> — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)

### The Problem

> "Most lawyers who try AI write something like 'review this contract' and get back something mediocre. Then they decide AI isn't useful for legal work. The problem is not the AI. The problem is the input."
> — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)

### What to Cover

1. **Templates are commodities, judgment isn't** — Everyone in your industry has the same templates. What differentiates you is what you *do* with them.

   > "A template library is not a competitive advantage. Every competent firm in your practice area has roughly the same templates... The thing that differentiates a great lawyer from a mediocre one was never the template. It was what the lawyer did with the template: how they spotted the issue the other side buried in Section 14(c), how they knew which indemnification fight was worth having and which to concede."
   > — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)

2. **The before/after** — Show the difference between a generic prompt and a skill-encoded prompt. The lawyer's own example:
   - Bad: "review this contract"
   - Good: "review this services agreement from the vendor's perspective. Flag provisions where the customer shifted risk beyond market norms. Check for missing provisions including limitation of liability, IP ownership, data handling, and termination for convenience. Produce a severity-rated summary with specific counter-language for each high-severity issue."

   > "The entire gap between 'AI is a toy' and 'AI changed my practice' lives in the quality of your instructions."
   > — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)

3. **Build a real skill live** — Take a business task the viewer does weekly (e.g. reviewing proposals, writing client updates, processing applications) and build a skill that encodes *your* analytical framework, preferred format, voice, and judgment. Write it once, it fires every time.

4. **Experienced practitioners have the biggest advantage** —

   > "Experienced lawyers have an enormous advantage in this new world, and most of them don't realize it. If you've spent 10 or 20 years developing judgment in your practice area, you are sitting on exactly the asset that AI makes more valuable, not less."
   > — [Zack Shapiro (@zackbshapiro)](https://x.com/zackbshapiro/status/2027389987444957625)
