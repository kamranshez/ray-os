Stub for the echo chamber segment. The named failure mode of every compounding loop, and the two antidotes.

## Thesis

You build a loop. The loop works. Six weeks later, every output sounds the same. Your discovery loop surfaces ideas, your drafting loop writes them, your scoring loop ranks them, your publishing loop ships them. Each loop is training on the prior loop's output. The system converges to a fixed point.

This is the failure mode every compounding loop is most prone to. It is silent. It feels like consistency. It is collapse.

There are two antidotes. You need both.

## Antidote one: exogenous signal injection

Pick one stage in your loop. Inject signal from outside the loop's own outputs at that stage. Not every stage. Exactly one.

For a content pipeline, the discovery stage. The L5 triager reads from the *world*, not from your prior posts. Your own posts are not a signal to the triager.

For a code generation loop, the verification stage. The eval suite is curated by humans, not generated by the loop.

For a sales outreach loop, the response data. Reply rates come from real prospects, not from a simulated grader.

One exogenous stage is enough. More than one and the loop loses coherence. None and it converges to slop.

## Antidote two: rubric drift detection

Every rubric the loop uses needs a freshness date. Quarterly, take twenty recent loop outputs. Have a human grade them blind, without seeing the loop's grade. Compare.

If the human and the loop disagree on more than 15% of cases, the rubric is stale. Rewrite the rubric. Do not just swap the model. Model swaps don't fix rubric drift, they hide it.

## Key beats

- The fixed-point collapse: same outputs forever, feels like consistency, is actually death.
- Why convergence is the *price* of self-improvement, not a bug. Every Curator (segment 4.1) tightens the rubric. Tighter rubric, narrower outputs. Narrower outputs, tighter rubric. Loop.
- The two-antidote framing as the standing rule. Don't ship a compounding loop without both.
- The diagnostic: take three of your loop's recent outputs and put them in front of an old colleague. If they can't tell which loop produced which, you have collapse.
- Connection back: Rippling's bug triage loop (4.2) works because failing production traces are exogenous signal. They come from real users, not from the loop's prior outputs.

## What this segment is *not*

Not "diversity is good." That's a slogan, not a mechanism. The mechanism is *where the exogenous signal enters*, *how often it enters*, and *how you detect drift in the rubric that filters it*.
