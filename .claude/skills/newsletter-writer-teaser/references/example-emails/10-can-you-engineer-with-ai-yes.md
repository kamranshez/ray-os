By now, you’ve likely heard more than enough about the potential risks associated with AI coding agents:

* LLMs are non-deterministic, making quality unpredictable
* It can output code that superficially looks good but is faulty or insecure
* The allure of speed can create piles of technical debt
* If left unattended, your agent can go rogue and wreck code, drop a database, or even delete your home directory
* Heavy use can leave you with mentally exhausted or worse, atrophy your skills and understanding of the codebase.

After months of coding with AI, I can confirm that these risks are real.

But I can confidently say that these risks are not inevitable.

And now that I’ve worked with 2500+ other developers to teach them my approach to AI engineering, I’m even more confident that you can make these problems a thing of the past.

My “secret weapon” for working with AI has been leaning into my Real Engineering skills, rather than abandoning them.

--------------------------------------
All engineering is about managing risk
--------------------------------------

If you think about it, nearly every tool or environment that engineers use or design for are innately dangerous.

Bridges have to handle heavy load, earthquakes, water, and wind.

Supply chains can be fragile. Electronics are delicate and easily ruined during fabrication.

Operating systems can crash, corrupt data, or be hacked.

Dealing with risk is in the job description for all engineers, whether you're building a toaster oven, skyscraper, or software.

When you approach AI Coding like a Real Engineer, part of the skillset is knowing how to mitigate, design around, and avoid these risks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
So, what do AI engineers do to ensure success and eliminate failure?
--------------------------------------------------------------------

They think like an engineer at every step of the process.

Beginning: A Real Engineer starts with a clear picture of what they're doing before they set out to do it. What success looks like, where the pitfalls lie.

Middle: A Real Engineer proceeds with a deep understanding of how their tools work, where they excel, where they're only middling, where they fail.

End: A Real Engineer creates systems to prevent mistakes before they happen, and ensures quality after the fact, too, because even human work can be flawed.

Through this lens, Real Engineering with AI might start to sound more familiar than you’d expect:

* Planning and grilling to give you, your team, and your coding agent a clear picture of not just what but how to implement
* Effective prototyping to try out a bunch of interfaces options and structural approaches before committing to a specific path
* Looping and iterating since we know the first result is rarely the best and can be improved
* Context management and handoffs to ensure your coding agent stays in “the smart zone”
* Comprehensive and automatic testing to ensure the code meets standards and broken changes can’t make it to production
* Permissions and security to protect your data and production environments from agent mistakes

The exact techniques change a bit with a coding agent in the mix, but the fundamentals are tried and true.

------------------------------------------------
The biggest changes were nothing like I expected
------------------------------------------------

I’m not bought into the hype, despite what the YouTube comments might say. And I absolutely don’t believe that AI is a career-ending event for devs like you and me.

Quite the opposite, in fact.

But using AI to write software every day for months has changed my opinions and, in a lot of ways, rewired the way I think about the entire software engineering process.

Instead of paying less attention overall, I find myself paying more attention in different areas than I ever did before.

Putting attention in the right places has helped me create volumes of production-grade code, in some cases code that’s even better than when I wrote it all myself.

The other unexpected result is what it’s done for all those someday-maybe projects and tasks that have cluttered up my brain for years.

So many things have gone from “ugh I guess I should start that” to “I can’t wait to start!” or “Finally Shipped!”

It's not an exaggeration to say it's changed my life as a software engineer, and I want to share that experience with you.
