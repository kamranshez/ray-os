## Master Summary: Winning Patterns

Last blind reconciliation: 2026-06-12 — patterns re-derived independently from raw V1–V31 results, diffed against this doc, contradictions approved and applied by Ray same day. Evidence tags: PATTERN = 3+ videos, TENDENCY = 2 videos / 2+ rounds on one video, ANECDOTE = 1 data point.

### Durable insights (2026-06-12 re-derivation TL;DR)

> This is a **digest of the tables and rules below** — a fast path, not separate findings. Every item here is backed by the ranked tables, Key Rules, and Test Mechanics later in this file (and by `_anti-patterns.md`). If you need evidence/citations, scroll down; if you just need the takeaway, stay here.

The ten cross-cutting takeaways from the blind V1–V31 re-analysis:

1. **Literal mechanism-description titles are the single worst category** — the title that explains what the feature literally does finishes last by 10–18pts every time it meets a stakes frame (V31 22.4%, V22 23.8%, V27-workflows 19.6%). Lead with stakes; let the thumbnail carry the "what."
2. **"Going Viral Right Now" momentum framing is 3-for-3** (May–Jun 2026, 36/37/40%) — but only on B-tier topics so far. Hot, possibly fast-burning; watch for fatigue.
3. **Verbatim title reuse fails on TOPIC mismatch, not fatigue** — "Biggest Subagent Upgrade Yet" won V25 (Apr) + V31 (Jun) but placed last on V28 (workflows video, May). Reuse winners only on topic-matched videos.
4. **Secrecy words ("Secret"/"Secretly"/"Hidden") lose** (last 3/3: V18×2, V19); usefulness-surprise ("Nobody Knew They Needed") wins. They feel identical but aren't.
5. **A number's POSITION matters more than the number** — "1,600 Hours" killed titles, then won as thumbnail text the same week (V11). Money/% numbers win in thumbnails ("96% Cheaper" ×2) but only tie in titles.
6. **Annotated compositions beat plain folder icons since V20** (V22 command-list 41.1% vs folder 30.5%). Folders won in March, lost every head-to-head after.
7. **Control decay is routine** — R1 winners regularly drop/lose on re-test (V23 39→31.4, V25 36.9→33.8). Don't lock after one round; don't panic at a small control dip.
8. **Incumbent thumbnails beat mid-flight text remixes** — only a different composition CLASS has dethroned one (V22 before/after diagram). Text tweaks don't.
9. **Within-test share ≠ video success** — V15 locked the archive's highest title share (49.4%) and still underperformed views at every checkpoint. Topic class sets the ceiling; titles only redistribute within it. Never cite share alone as a win.
10. **Numbers: credential > inventory count > effort-hours**; faceless thumbnails are NOT a hard anti-pattern (won V20, V17 R2 — composition-dependent); "shorter titles win" is false (winners span 5–11 words). All three retired/reconciled 2026-06-12.

### Key Rules

**Titles:**
1. "Anthropic" authority framing is reliable but not unbeatable (V23 R2 and V19: Claude Code-subject titles beat it when the feature itself is the star)
2. "Dropped" > "Added" > "Changed" — casual hype > newness > improvement. Cleanest head-to-head: V10 R5, "Just Dropped..." 42.3% vs "Anthropic Reveals..." 29.5% vs "Anthropic Just Added..." 28.2%
3. Numbers in titles: credentials ("Top 0.01%") help; inventory counts are neutral-to-negative ("10 New" won V9, "18 Unreleased" last V19, "60 Tips" last vs count-free twin 23.4% vs 45.6% V11 R4); effort-hours are negative — move the number to the thumbnail (replaced the old "specific numbers boost engagement" rule, 2026-06-12)
4. Title length is not a lever — winners range from 5 to 11 words (V22's 44% auto-win was 11 words; V19 R1's winner was 10). Optimize the frame, not the character count (replaced the old "shorter titles win" rule, 2026-06-12)
5. Listing multiple features in the title kills performance (V5 26%, V3 29.7%)
6. "Explained" educational framing consistently underperforms (V5 26%, V7 32.8%, V8 29.5%)
7. Catchy terminology ("Swarms") beats technical ("Multi-Agent Teams") (V8 36.4% vs 34.1%, ANECDOTE)
8. Specific tool names beat generic terms ("Claude Code" > "AI Coding") (V4)
9. Pipe format ("| Here's What Changed") underperforms — avoid (V9 31.4%, V3 29.7%, V26 26.1%)
10. **Literal mechanism description is the biggest single loser** — the arm that explains what the feature literally does finishes last by 10–18 pts: V31 R2 "Subagents Can Now Spawn Their Own Subagents" 22.4%, V22 R2 "Now Asks a Stronger Model When It Gets Confused" 23.8%, V27-workflows R1 "Hasn't Announced This Feature Yet" 19.6% (worst in archive). Stakes/significance framing beats mechanism every time they meet. (PATTERN)
11. First-person casual loses on feature videos — V10 R2 25.4%, V27-workflows R2 "my new favourite thing" 23.5%, V19 R3 30.6%, all last. Exception: personal-workflow videos where Ray is the subject (V4 won at 36.1%). (PATTERN)
12. Secrecy words ("Secret", "Secretly", "Hidden") lose; usefulness-surprise wins. Secrecy placed last 3× (V18 R1 28.8%, V18 R2 26.9%, V19 R3 30.6%). "Nobody Knew They Needed" is a usefulness claim, not a secrecy claim — don't conflate them. (TENDENCY)
13. Verbatim reuse of a winning title on a NEW video works when topic-matched, fails when transplanted: "Biggest Subagent Upgrade Yet" won V25 (Apr) and V31 (Jun) but placed last on V28 (workflows topic, 30.3%). Topic mismatch is more dangerous than fatigue. (PATTERN)

**Thumbnails:**
1. "How I [X] Now" holds the archive record (51.5%) but is a personal-video formula, n=1 from Jan 2026 — for feature videos, name the feature instead (see formula table)
2. Shorter is better (2-3 words ideal)
3. Present tense "Now" beats year references (V4)
4. Personal framing ("How I") beats feature framing ("How [Feature]") (V4 51.5% vs V6 32.2% — cross-video comparison, both personal-adjacent topics)
5. Simple past tense works ("Changed", "Dropped")
6. **Annotated diagram/command-list compositions beat plain folder icons from V20 onward**: V22 command-list 41.1% vs folder 30.5%; V23 slash-list won; V24 browser+pills 39% won; V25 browser composition 35.1% vs folder 33.6%; V20 faceless "Plan Mode 2.0." beat the /ultraplan folder 38.5% vs 35.3%. Folder icons won in March (V18, V19) — composition richness is the current edge. (PATTERN)
7. Concrete value numbers win in the THUMBNAIL position even when the same numbers fail in titles ("It's 96% Cheaper!" ×2 on V13; "1,600 Hours" on V11). (TENDENCY)
8. Face vs faceless is MIXED, not a law: faceless WON V20 thumb R1 (38.5%, YouTube auto-declared) and V17 R2 (34.3%); faceless lost V13 R1, V22 (last), V25 (last). Composition quality decides, not face presence. Default to face, but a strong faceless concept is a legitimate test arm — the old V13 hard anti-pattern is retired (2026-06-12). (TENDENCY each way)
9. Expression: subtly-impressed closed-mouth beat open-mouth smile (40.3% vs 31.2%) and held as winner the next round (36.6%) — V23 R2+R3, one video, two consistent rounds. (TENDENCY)
10. Incumbent thumbnails usually beat mid-flight challengers (V30-loops R4: original 38.8% vs fresh challengers 30.9%/30.3%). A challenger must be a different composition CLASS to dethrone — V22 thumb R2's before/after diagram did beat the R1 command-list winner. Text remixes don't. (TENDENCY)

### Title ↔ Thumbnail Division of Labor (PATTERN)

Thumbnail names the THING (/dream, /leaked, command list, "Nested Subagents"); title carries the stakes/social proof ("Feature Nobody Knew They Needed", "Makes Sonnet Feel Like Opus", "Biggest Subagent Upgrade Yet"). Winning packages: V18, V19, V22, V31. The inverse — title names the mechanism while the thumbnail carries stakes — never won a round in the archive. One exception on record: V12 ran near-redundant "Skills 2.0?" + "...Skills 2.0" and won anyway; the ?-asks/title-confirms structure may explain it (n=1, don't generalize).

### Test Mechanics (how to read rounds)

1. **Spread size tells you whether the round tested anything.** Three same-family paraphrases → ~33/33/33 noise (V16 R3 at 33.4/33.4/33.2, V17 R1, V25 R3). Arms differing in KIND → 12–20 pt spreads (V14 R2, V27-workflows R1, V11 R4). Structure rounds across frame families, not paraphrases. (PATTERN)
2. **Control decay is routine.** R1 winners frequently lose later rounds to fresh challengers (V11, V23 39%→31.4%, V25 36.9%→33.8%, V27-workflows, V29 37.1%→31%). Don't treat an R1 win as final; don't panic when a control drops a few points on re-test. (PATTERN)
3. **YouTube auto-declared winners cluster at ≥7 pt leads and none has been reversed** (V14, V18 ×2, V20 thumb, V22, V27-workflows, V31). (TENDENCY)
4. **Within-test share ≠ video success.** V15 locked a 49.4% winner — the highest title share in the archive — and still underperformed typical views at every checkpoint. Topic class sets the ceiling; titles only redistribute within it (V11, V15, V24, V26, V29, V30). Never cite share alone as success. (PATTERN — the most consequential rule in this file)
