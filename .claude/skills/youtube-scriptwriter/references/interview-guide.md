# Interview guide

Two rounds of AskUserQuestion. Round 1 picks the angle; Round 2 fills only the ingredient gaps. Ray dictates his answers, so expect speech-to-text noise and interpret intent. The whole point of interviewing instead of just writing: the winning ingredients (receipts, demo details, verdicts) exist only in Ray's head, and a script built without them is a Tier 4 explainer no matter how well it is written.

## Round 1: the angle

Derive 2-4 genuinely different angles from the messy thoughts. "Genuinely different" means they would produce different videos, not the same video with different titles. Typical spread for one piece of material:

- the urgency/event angle (what deadline or moment makes this NOW?)
- the insider/discovery angle (what did Ray find that nobody else can show?)
- the workflow/identity angle (how does this change how Ray actually works?)
- the news angle (only if the underlying release is genuinely big)

Present them in ONE AskUserQuestion call. Each option:

- **label**: short angle name (e.g. "Deadline strategy", "Insider discovery")
- **description**: which format tier it maps to and one performance fact from the playbook that justifies it
- **preview**: a title direction plus a 2-line hook sketch, so Ray is choosing between concrete videos, not abstract categories

Put the recommended angle first with "(Recommended)". If the messy thoughts already clearly commit to an angle, still confirm it in Round 1 but fold in the most important Round 2 question alongside (AskUserQuestion allows up to 4 questions per call).

## Round 2: the eight ingredients

Check each ingredient against the messy thoughts plus the Round 1 answer. Ask only about real gaps, up to 4 questions in one call; a second call only if something load-bearing is still missing. Skip ingredients the chosen angle does not need (a trust-sell tutorial needs no urgency wrapper).

1. **Stakes wrapper** — what is time-sensitive or exclusive? A deadline, an event, a window, a discovery nobody has covered?
2. **Insider receipts** — what evidence exists that nobody else can show? Binary dig, extracted prompt, experiment with an N, named person, hard number. This is the differentiation engine; if the answer is "nothing", the video needs an experiment or dig before it needs a script, and it is worth saying so.
3. **The demo** — which real project does it run on, and what is the exact prompt? Is there wait time to teach over? Demos on Ray's real products (Hyperwhisper, RayOS, the class) are the corpus norm; a toy example is a downgrade.
4. **Transferable principle** — what does the viewer keep even if they cannot access the feature or tool?
5. **Personal verdict** — will Ray actually use it? What failed, disappointed, or surprised him? Honest limitations are a trust builder, not a weakness.
6. **Analogy spine** — what is this like? One analogy sustained through the video beats five one-offs. If Ray has none, propose 2-3 and let him pick.
7. **Mid-video payoff** — what result can be teased in the first third and paid off at 40-60%? (Retention danger zone; see the playbook.)
8. **CTA register** — hard sell (which offer, what urgency, where it can be woven into the demo) or trust sell (no pitch, newsletter only)?

Question-writing rules:

- Offer concrete options extracted from the messy thoughts, not open-ended "what do you think?" prompts. Ray should mostly be picking and correcting, not composing.
- Any ingredient already present in the messy thoughts gets restated in the draft, not re-asked in the interview.
- If Ray answers "skip it" or picks Other with a terse note, take the hint and move to drafting. A good-enough interview plus a draft beats a perfect interview with no script.

## After the interview

Restate the plan in one short paragraph (angle, hook idea, demo, payoff placement, CTA register) as part of the message flow, then go straight into drafting. Do not ask "shall I write it now?".
