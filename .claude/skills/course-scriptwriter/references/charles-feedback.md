# Charles Bell — Audience Feedback (4 Mar 2026)

Source: Email thread between Charles Bell and Ray Amjad.

Charles leads a team of established C programmers (microcontrollers background) trying to learn AI coding tools. His team burned ~200 hours each trying to absorb content from fast-paced tutorial videos.

## Key Problems Identified

1. **Pace too fast** — Team had to pause 10-20 times per video and repeatedly back up
2. **Screens flash by** — No time to read what's on screen or process highlighted areas
3. **Editing removes natural pauses** — No breathing room for the audience to think
4. **Jargon without context** — Terms like "CLT" instead of "command line terminal"; assumes familiarity
5. **Feature showcasing without teaching** — Most creators talk about what a tool *can* do without showing *how* to actually do it
6. **No hand-holding** — Content doesn't help people onto the first rungs of the learning ladder

## What Charles's Team Wants

1. **Real-world problem first** — A discussion that makes the problem understood (e.g., "I am buried in emails", "calculating propulsion")
2. **Visual aids** — Line art illustrations to explain the logic
3. **Implementation steps** — A 5-10 minute walkthrough showing the actual setup and implementation
4. **Technical context** — Showing the tool running on specific systems (Mac, Windows 11, or Linux) and explaining why
5. **The result** — Showing the solution actually running and working

## Specific Suggestions

1. Slow down delivery: 50% reduction in speech rate
2. Visual lingering: Screens visible for at least a few seconds
3. Restore natural pauses: Stop editing out breath steps
4. Signposting: Titles and clear spacing between sections

## Key Quotes

> "We have not moved forward with a purchase yet because my team found it impossible to absorb the information without pausing 10 to 20 times per video and repeatedly backing up."

> "While your content and logic are the best available, the lack of 'breathing space' and the speed at which screens flash by robs the experience of any joy."

> "Almost no one provides this level of detail or 'hand-holding,' which is exactly what is needed to get people onto the first few rungs of the learning ladder. Without it, you're just showcasing features for people who already understand the subject."

> "Most videos just throw 'agents' and 'skills' at the viewer but never engage in the paired programming that walks someone through the process. That 'walk and talk' is where the actual knowledge transfer happens."

> "We need content that speaks to the 'dummies in the back row,' uses clear terms like 'command line terminal' instead of 'CLT,' and functions as a door opener."

> "My team—mostly established C programmers with years of experience in microcontrollers—has burned about 200 hours each trying to get our heads around this."
