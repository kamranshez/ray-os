# Ray's Voice Guide — Course Scripts

## How Ray opens a lesson

No hook formula. No "what's up guys." Just state the thing:

- "So Claude Code just added a feature that most people don't know about yet."
- "So Anthropic just updated skill-creator — the thing you use to build skills."
- "So the Anthropic team just published the highest signal article you'll see this week."

Pattern: "So [thing] just [happened/exists]." Then immediately into why it matters.

For course lessons (not YouTube), the opener can also reference the prior lesson:
- "In the last video we built the interrogate skill. Now we're going to build something more complex."
- "So we've got our brand context set up. The question now is — does it actually make a difference?"

## Sentence patterns Ray uses

**Starting sentences with "And" and "So" and "Now":**
- "And here's what's interesting."
- "And this is exactly why [thing] matters."
- "So the question becomes — [question]?"
- "Now here's the thing."
- "Now if you've used Claude Code for any meaningful amount of time..."

**The "that's [simple restatement]" pattern:**
- "Same idea as a teacher grading an exam — here's the question, here's what a good answer includes, did the student hit the marks? That's it."
- "You tell it your brand voice, your process, and your preferences just once, and it remembers them every single time. That's what skills do."

**Thinking out loud:**
- "So the question becomes — if memory is essential but the memory keeps getting worse... how do you fix that?"
- "And I actually talked about this a while back —"
- "Here's why this works."

**Direct second-person:**
- "You'd never know."
- "You just describe what you want and Claude figures out how."
- "That's a different kind of test."

## How Ray explains concepts

He goes: simple statement → analogy or example → "that's it" or restatement.

Example from auto-dream:
"An 'eval' is just a test case. You give AI a specific input, describe what the output should look like, and check whether it passes. Same idea as a teacher grading an exam — here's the question, here's what a good answer includes, did the student hit the marks? That's it."

Example from skills-2.0:
"Most people think a skill is a SKILL.md file. You write some instructions, save it, done. Thariq says that's the least interesting part. A skill is a folder."

Pattern: Misconception → correction → brief explanation.

## How Ray does demos

He narrates what's happening as it happens:

"So here's what Anthropic built. When auto-dream triggers, it spawns a background agent — a separate Claude instance — that receives this prompt:"

Then shows the thing. Then explains what it means:

"And then it walks through three phases. Phase 1 — Orient. First, it orients itself..."

Pattern: Setup what you're about to show → show it → walk through what happened.

## What Ray doesn't do

- No "hey guys" or "what's up everyone"
- No "without further ado" or "let's dive in" or "let's get started"
- No "as you can see" (just describe what's on screen)
- No "it's important to note that" (if it's important, just say it)
- No "let's go ahead and" (just do it)
- No filler transitions between sections — just start the next section
- No emoji in scripts
- No rhetorical questions that don't get answered
- No hedging ("I think maybe possibly this could perhaps...")
- Doesn't explain things he's going to explain later ("we'll get to that")
  — either explain it now or don't mention it

## Pacing

Ray's scripts read fast because they're dense. Every sentence either:
1. States a fact
2. Shows something
3. Explains why something matters

If a sentence doesn't do one of those three things, cut it.

## Tone for course vs YouTube

YouTube Ray has more energy and urgency — "most people don't know about this yet."
Course Ray is slightly more methodical — he knows the student is committed, so he
can slow down on the "why" without losing them. But he's still conversational and
direct. Never academic. Never stiff.

The biggest difference: YouTube scripts have a hook section (0:00-0:25) designed to
stop the scroll. Course scripts skip this entirely. Open with context, not a hook.
