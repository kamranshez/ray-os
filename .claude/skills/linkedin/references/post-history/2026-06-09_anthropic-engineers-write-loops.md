Most people open Claude Code and start typing prompts.

Anthropic's engineers open Claude Code and start writing loops.

Boris Cherny, the founder of Claude Code, said it plainly on a podcast last week:

"I don't prompt Claude anymore. I have loops that are running. They're the ones that are prompting Claude and kind of figuring out what to do. My job is to write loops."

He uninstalled his IDE in November. He runs around 200 Claudes in the background reading Twitter, sitting in Slack, triaging GitHub.

The new unit of work isn't a prompt. It's a loop.

→ A scheduled task that opens a PR for every Sentry issue affecting 20+ users
→ A morning routine that reads your competitors' changelogs and writes the spec
→ A goal that tests every user flow in your app overnight and hands you the failing ones
→ A loop that watches a Slack channel and acts on your emoji reactions

The engineers who move fastest aren't the best prompters.

They're the best loop designers.
