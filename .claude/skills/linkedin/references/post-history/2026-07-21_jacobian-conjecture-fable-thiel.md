Wild: an 87-year-old math conjecture just fell, and the mathematician credited an AI model as his collaborator.

The Jacobian conjecture had been open since 1939. Levent Alpoge (Morgan Prize winner, now at Anthropic) posted a counterexample this weekend. It fits in a single tweet:

→ A polynomial map from C^3 to C^3
→ Jacobian determinant: constant -2, never zero
→ Three different points that all land on (-1/4, 0, 0)

The conjecture says a map like that must be invertible. Three points hitting the same target means it is not. Anyone can check it by plugging in the numbers.

His credit line: "thanx to my close friend akhil for asking about it and my other close friend fable for working during the world cup final."

Fable is Anthropic's newest Claude model. The post is at 21.2M views.

Now the uncomfortable part. Peter Thiel was just asked whether AI hits word people or math people harder:

"My intuition would be it's going to be quite the opposite, where it seems much worse for the math people than the word people."

His argument: math is society's universal gating test. Med schools weed people out with physics and calculus, not surgical dexterity. Thiel himself had a chess bias in the early 90s and thought chess ability should gate everything. Deep Blue ended that in 1997.

"Isn't that what's going to happen to math?"

That means the scarce skill is shifting from solving problems to choosing them. Alpoge still needed the taste to ask the right question. Fable did the grinding, during a football match.

Taste survives automation. Grinding does not.
