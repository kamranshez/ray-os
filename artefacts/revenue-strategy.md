## Strategy 1: Improve Revenue from the Existing Class

These moves don't require re-recording anything. They're pricing, packaging, and distribution changes applied to what already exists.

### Pricing

- **Widen the lifetime/annual gap.** Lifetime at $202 vs annual at $199 cannibalizes recurring revenue — anyone rational picks lifetime. Raise lifetime to $399-499 while keeping annual at $199. Marie Poulin added $100 increments and saw zero drop in signups. Matt Pocock launched at $490 specifically to stay under the $500 corporate card threshold — that's your ceiling for self-serve.
- **Price test the annual.** Bump annual to $249. If conversion doesn't drop, it was underpriced. Mark Shust's course coach told him the same thing. Joel Hooks said the programming course market is "grossly undercharging by an order of magnitude relative to career value."
- **Make team pricing self-serve.** Right now it's "contact us." Turn it into a real product — 5-pack at 30% off, 10-pack at 40% off, 20-pack at 50% off. Companies have training budgets and procurement processes. A self-serve checkout converts faster than a "contact us" form. Kyle Shevlin called team licenses "an obvious way to increase revenue and distribution." Tyler McGinnis built his entire company around team plans.

### Email Capture & Distribution

- **Build an email list from the existing YouTube audience.** You have 206 videos on YouTube and no email capture. This is the single biggest gap. Every successful course creator in the badass.dev interviews attributes their launch revenue to their email list. Matt Pocock's $730K launch came from 18K email subscribers. Marie Poulin said 80% of her buyers found her through YouTube.
- **Create a gated lead magnet.** Take one compelling lesson (or create a "Claude Code in 30 Minutes" mini-tutorial) and gate it behind an email. Total TypeScript's approach: first problem video is free, first solution requires email. This grew Matt Pocock's list faster than anything else.
- **Launch a weekly newsletter.** Tyler McGinnis treats the Bytes newsletter as literally the #2 company priority after course content. It builds brand recall even among people who never buy. Wes Bos uses Syntax FM the same way — sells courses at the end, sponsors pay independently, and the audience grows the brand. A "Claude Code Weekly" covering changelog highlights + one tip + personality would serve this role. Even people who never subscribe stay aware you exist.

### Engagement & Retention

- **Add a 6-week check-in email.** Marie Poulin sends a simple email 6 weeks after purchase: "What's challenging you right now?" + "What's a win you had this week?" This produces: (1) content gap signals, (2) testimonials on autopilot, (3) re-engagement with lapsed students. Costs nothing to implement.
- **Enhanced transcripts.** Angie Jones found 60% of learners use transcripts alongside video, not as a replacement. Market the transcripts as a feature. Add code snippets and screenshots inline (not just raw speech-to-text). This is a perceived value increase with no new recording.
- **Gamification / completion certificates.** Angie Jones's TAU leaderboard drove competitive learners to take every course to keep their ranking. Certificates get shared on LinkedIn = free marketing. Even simple completion badges that students can screenshot and post would help. Marie Poulin's students share certificates organically.
- **Cross-link related lessons.** Kyle Shevlin's key insight: knowledge is a graph, not a line. If someone is watching the subagents lesson, link them to the explore subagent lesson and the memory lesson. Let advanced users navigate nonlinearly. This increases perceived depth and time-on-platform.

### Sales Page

- **Segment copy for different visitors.** Brennan Dunn built his own platform specifically because no hosted platform lets you show different CTAs to logged-in users vs. unknown visitors vs. lapsed subscribers. Even a simple version — different hero text for "new visitor" vs. "returning student" — converts better.
- **Add the "pain-dream-fix" structure** (Amy Hoy / Brennan Dunn). The current page leads with features (curriculum list). The badass.dev creators who convert best lead with: what's painful about not knowing this → what life looks like when you do → here's how the course gets you there. You have some of this ("This Is Already a Hiring Filter") but it could go deeper.
- **Expansion pack model.** Total TypeScript sells a Core Volume + React expansion pack + future packs. You could restructure: sell "Master Claude Code" (the main class) and then sell Prompt Engineering, Skills, Business as separate $99-149 add-ons OR as a bundle. Multiple entry points at multiple price points. Some people only want the Claude Code class. Some want everything. Don't force everyone through one door.

## Strategy 2: Future Courses (Pre-Revenue Before Recording)

The universal pattern from all 22 badass.dev interviews: **live first → feedback → record → launch to the list.** Every creator who skipped live delivery and went straight to recording had worse outcomes. Every creator who did live first made money during development AND produced better final content.

### Phase 1: Build the List (Ongoing, Start Now)

- **Weekly newsletter.** Even before the next course exists. This compounds. Tyler McGinnis spent years building Bytes before it became a revenue driver. Start with Claude Code changelog recaps + one weekly tip + your personality. Be funny — Tyler says humor is why Bytes works: "people love it because we don't take ourselves too seriously."
- **Free gated tutorials.** For each future class topic, create a single high-quality free tutorial and gate it behind email. Total TypeScript's Beginner's Tutorial drove massive list growth. This also validates interest — if nobody signs up for a free prompt engineering tutorial, maybe that's not the next course to build.
- **Consistent YouTube publishing.** Marie Poulin committed to one YouTube video per week for 12 weeks and went from unknown to "the Notion girl." Scott Tolinski's 340K YouTube subscribers were his foundation. You already do this — just make sure every video drives to the email list.

### Phase 2: Validate with Live Workshops ($15-30K per run)

- **Sell 30 seats at $500-1,200 for a half-day workshop.** Matt Pocock sold 30 seats at $1,200 (with early bird discount) for his first workshop — $15K in 2 weeks of promotion. Brennan Dunn ran his consultancy masterclass 15 times live. Kent C. Dodds did 8 live workshops for Epic React. The pattern is universal.
- **Use the workshop to discover confusion points.** Matt Pocock: "I got it wrong in terms of the amount of spoonfeeding that people want." He only discovered this through live interaction. Brennan Dunn: "The 15th class got the best version." You cannot get this feedback from a recorded course.
- **The live version is the product AND the research.** You're getting paid to develop the curriculum. Marie Poulin pre-sold with zero content ready: "We're going to co-create this. I'm going to build it based on your needs." $10K in the first week.
- **Rob Fitzpatrick's Workshop Survival Guide format:** 10-min intro → discussion → action items in a Google doc → async drip follow-up Loom videos over 2 months. Brennan Dunn swears by this. The live event handles the "why," the async follow-up handles the "how."

### Phase 3: Record the Polished Version

- **Only record after 2-3 live runs.** Kent C. Dodds realized after live testing that what he thought was one workshop was actually four. He expanded it and added exercises. If he'd gone straight to recording, the structure would have been wrong.
- **Record unscripted.** Matt Pocock records off-the-cuff after having a clear plan for curriculum structure. "It has been a shock to me how well people respond to essentially me just reacting to stuff in code." His dramatic training helps, but the key insight is: plan the structure rigorously, then deliver naturally. Skip scripting.
- **"Example-first" over "concept-first."** Matt Pocock's biggest teaching shift: start with complex code, let learners struggle, then explain. "You're understanding exactly the problem that this information is designed to solve, so the information feels more valuable to you." Kent C. Dodds does the same: introduce problem → exercise → walk through solution.

### Phase 4: Launch to the List

- **Segment the list before launch.** Total TypeScript surveyed their 18K list and found 1,000 people at the right skill level. Sent early access to just those 1,000. 34 bought immediately. This soft launch catches edge cases before the full announcement.
- **Just-in-time launch emails.** Total TypeScript wrote each launch email the day before it was sent, based on questions and objections coming in from learners that day. This is live A/B testing of your sales copy in real time.
- **Pre-release at a discount, full launch at full price.** Total TypeScript pre-release: $490 (40% off $800). Full launch later: $590 with up to 20% discount. The pre-release rewards early believers and generates social proof for the full launch.
- **Anchor price against the live workshop.** If people paid $1,200 for the live version, the recorded version at $490-590 feels like a bargain. The live workshops establish the value ceiling. This is why the live-first strategy matters even beyond the content feedback — it sets the price anchor.

### Phase 5: Expand with Modules (Ongoing Revenue)

- **Expansion pack model.** Total TypeScript Core Volume + React expansion + future packs. Each new module generates a new launch cycle. First expansion (React) made $194K. "Adding more paid modules onto an existing course always feels a bit risky — but it validates the idea that people want more specialised content."
- **Paid beta for each new module.** Shift Nudge sold 100 paid beta slots before the course existed. Paid testers give dramatically better feedback than free ones because they're invested. Charge $199 for 100 beta slots on each new class = $20K before recording starts.
- **Consider guest instructors for expansion.** Scott Tolinski pays guest instructors based on stream revenue share (inspired by Ghostly International's 60% royalty model). This lets you expand the catalog without recording everything yourself. Total TypeScript is also exploring bringing in other instructors for sustainability.

## The Revenue Formula (Distilled from 22 Interviews)

```
Free content → Email list → Live workshops (paid) → Record polished version → Launch to list → Expand with modules
     ↑                                                                                              |
     └──────────────────────────────── newsletter keeps the flywheel spinning ──────────────────────┘
```

Every step generates revenue or compounds future revenue. The creators who skip steps (especially the email list and live workshops) consistently underperform.
