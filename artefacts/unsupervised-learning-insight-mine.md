### 1. Bitter Lesson Engineering: stop telling your AI HOW, tell it WHAT
**Core insight.** The biggest lever in harness quality is whether your scaffolding micromanages
execution or describes intent. Prescriptive step-by-step instructions ("copy this file, then load
that, then do X") do not just age, they actively make a strong model *dumber* than it would be
unguided, a state he calls "BLE-hobbled." Good harness engineering loads dense context about who you
are, your goals, your taste, and what good and bad outcomes look like, then hands the best model the
best tools and gets out of the way. The inversion point (telling-how stopped helping) landed around
2025. He runs a dedicated "BLE skill" against his whole harness to audit for it.
**Original source.** [Good and Bad Harness Engineering](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/good-and-bad-harness-engineering) and [Bitter Lesson Engineering](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/bitter-lesson-engineering), rooted in Sutton's [The Bitter Lesson](https://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html).
**Relates to.** New ground and a sequel. Your "Loopy AI" class frames a Layer 1 harness as a
substrate, but nobody teaches the harness as a *design discipline*. A whole video can be "refactor a
prescriptive CLAUDE.md into a Bitter-Lesson one: is this line a preference or a procedure?"
**Creator's video.** [We're All Building a Single Digital Assistant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUForkn00mk)

### 2. The Articulation Gap: capture, not capability, is the real bottleneck
**Core insight.** The barrier between human and AI expertise is not capability, it is *capture*.
Experts know things they cannot explain ("20 years of pattern recognition locked in their heads,
ask them why and they say 'I just knew'"). The moment that tacit expertise is written down as a
skill, SOP, or example, AI executes it instantly. He pairs this with an "expertise ratchet": once
captured into a skill or open-source repo, expertise never leaves the pool and lifts every AI
instance at once.
**Original source.** [Exactly Why and How AI Will Replace Knowledge Work](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/exactly-why-and-how-ai-will-replace-knowledge-work).
**Relates to.** Reframes already-covered skills and spec material. Your course teaches the mechanics
(skills, spec-developer); this gives them a sharp *why*: the craft of agentic coding is closing the
articulation gap, turning "I just knew" into an executable file.
**Creator's video.** [AI WILL Replace Knowledge Workers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g3SrYIUv-OE)

### 3. Intelligence = Model + Scaffolding, and the seven-component architecture
**Core insight.** Real-world system intelligence is the model multiplied by its scaffolding, and the
scaffolding now dominates. Any serious personal or agentic AI system converges on seven competable
components: Intelligence, Context, Personality, Tools, Security, Orchestration, Interface. PAI,
Claude Code, OpenCode, and MoltBot are arriving at this blueprint independently, which is itself
evidence the architecture is right. Each component maps onto a concrete Claude Code primitive
(Orchestration = hooks across lifecycle events plus subagents; Tools = skills plus MCP plus Fabric).
**Original source.** [Personal AI Infrastructure](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/personal-ai-infrastructure).
**Relates to.** New ground. The personal-infrastructure framing is absent from your curriculum, and
this gives the whole course a clean skeleton: one module per component, each grounded in a Claude
Code feature.
**Creator's video.** [We're All Building a Single Digital Assistant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUForkn00mk) and [How and Why I Built PAI, with Nathan Labenz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvXC7sqso4w)

### 4. The Algorithm: generalized hill-climbing via Ideal State Criteria
**Core insight.** You cannot hill-climb toward something you cannot test, so the whole game is
reverse-engineering a request into short, discrete, boolean, testable "Ideal State Criteria," which
double as the verification criteria. Two nested loops drive everything: an outer CURRENT STATE to
IDEAL STATE loop wrapping an inner scientific-method loop (observe, think, plan, build, execute,
verify, learn). It runs on top of Claude Code's Task system and scales criteria count with effort
(4 to 8 for a fix, 40 to 150-plus for a project). It is an explicit attempt to generalize Geoffrey
Huntley's Ralph loop beyond code.
**Original source.** [The Last Algorithm](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/the-last-algorithm) and [Nobody Is Talking About Generalized Hill-Climbing](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/nobody-is-talking-about-generalized-hill-climbing).
**Relates to.** Sequel to your Loopy AI "Designing a Task Lifecycle." The observe-to-verify structure
is a buildable skill that connects spec writing and verifiable acceptance criteria into one workflow.
**Creator's video.** [We're All Building a Single Digital Assistant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUForkn00mk)

### 5. AI does autocomplete for answers, not text (the fake-physics test)
**Core insight.** The "it is just next-token prediction" dismissal misses *which* text is predicted:
the next token of the *answer to your question*. He proves understanding versus regurgitation with a
falsifiable test: whodunit scenarios with invented physics (a "waking stone" that glows only while
you are awake) that cannot be in training data. A fresh model solves them, and with thinking on you
watch it reason through the made-up rules to the right killer. He splits "understanding" into
functional (AI clearly has it) versus experiential (no evidence either way).
**Original source.** [AI Predicts the Text of Answers](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/ai-predicts-answers) (live demo at aiunderstands.ai).
**Relates to.** New ground. Your curriculum has no "how the model actually works" explainer, and this
doubles as a reusable eval methodology: test reasoning versus memorization by building
out-of-distribution problems.
**Creator's video.** [AI Predicts The Text Of Answers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kvwcj9q1Bt0)

### 6. Verifiability is the universal ladder (Karpathy's Software 2.0, generalized)
**Core insight.** Karpathy framed Software 1.0 as writing software and Software 2.0 as verifying it.
Miessler's move: why restrict verification to code? Your Ideal State criteria are simultaneously your
goal and your verifier, so the same measurable list lets you hill-climb on anything. Without a
verifiable ideal state, an agentic loop has nothing to climb toward.
**Original source.** [The Last Algorithm](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/the-last-algorithm), citing [Karpathy, Software 2.0](https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35).
**Relates to.** Pairs with idea 4 as its conceptual underpinning. Reframes "write tests and acceptance
criteria" from afterthought to the core enabling skill of agentic work.
**Creator's video.** [We're All Building a Single Digital Assistant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUForkn00mk)

### 7. AGI arrives as scaffolding, not as a model (Claude Code is proto-AGI)
**Core insight.** Miessler defines AGI operationally as "a system that can replace an average human
knowledge worker" and argues it lands via the *harness*, not a smarter base model. It does not matter
whether a true general intelligence does the work or "57 AIs in a trench coat" stitched by
scaffolding. His framing: ChatGPT was the breakthrough for knowledge, Claude Code is the equivalent
for action, and the gap to replacing an employee is "more wiring and plumbing," not a new theory.
Evidence: Anthropic's CoWork was reportedly built in a week, by Claude Code, with no human writing
code. His prediction for the drop-in-knowledge-worker threshold: 2027.
**Original source.** [Claude Code Is Proto-AGI](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/claude-code-proto-agi) and the [Nathan Labenz interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvXC7sqso4w).
**Relates to.** The thesis that motivates the entire course: students are not waiting for a better
model, they are building the scaffold that closes the gap now.
**Creator's video.** [How and Why I Built PAI, with Nathan Labenz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvXC7sqso4w)

### 8. Effort tiers: gate harness depth so 90% of tasks stay fast
**Core insight.** A general agent that runs full multi-phase machinery on every request is unusable,
so The Algorithm assigns an effort tier during the observe phase (Instant under 10s, Fast under 1min,
Standard under 2min, up to Comprehensive under 120min). The tier gates everything downstream: how
many criteria to generate, whether to enter Plan Mode, which capabilities to engage (subagents,
Council debate, RedTeam, git-worktree experiments, browser verification), and how thorough
verification must be.
**Original source.** [Nobody Is Talking About Generalized Hill-Climbing](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/nobody-is-talking-about-generalized-hill-climbing).
**Relates to.** New and rarely taught. Answers a real student pain ("my agent over-engineers a typo
fix"). The capability-matrix-gated-by-effort idea is implementable today.
**Creator's video.** [We're All Building a Single Digital Assistant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUForkn00mk)

### 9. The file system is your memory (skip the RAG and memory-vendor stack)
**Core insight.** Miessler comes down "firmly team file system": markdown files under a
`.claude/MEMORY/` tree plus a JSONL of every Claude Code transcript event are his storage, memory,
and context management. He dislikes RAG as "lossy," reserving it for his 10,000-post archive only.
The payoff is portability: he moved PAI to OpenCode for two weeks in a single day because "the
platform is just markdown files, skills, MCPs, context files."
**Original source.** [Personal AI Infrastructure](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/personal-ai-infrastructure) and the [Labenz interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvXC7sqso4w).
**Relates to.** Contrast and a "build it tonight" episode. Counters the "I need a vector DB" instinct
that trips up beginners. Durable agent memory with nothing but files and hooks, on-brand for Claude
Code.
**Creator's video.** [How and Why I Built PAI, with Nathan Labenz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvXC7sqso4w)

### 10. Your company is a Graph of Algorithms (and AI reads the whole graph)
**Core insight.** Every company is a graph of interconnected algorithms executed mostly by humans,
where each workflow decomposes into discrete steps and each step decomposes further, "algorithms all
the way down." Once articulated this way, waste and redundancy become visible and automatable.
**Original source.** [Companies Are Just a Graph of Algorithms](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/companies-graph-of-algorithms).
**Relates to.** This is task decomposition and subagent orchestration at org scale. Teaching devs to
see a workflow as a DAG of discrete, chainable tasks is exactly how you design multi-agent pipelines,
MCP tool graphs, and skill chains.
**Creator's video.** [AI WILL Replace Knowledge Workers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g3SrYIUv-OE)

### 11. Modular, auto-assembled context (numbered components, build script, LATEST pointer)
**Core insight.** PAI's behavior lives in a `SKILL.md` auto-assembled from numbered modular markdown
components (`00-frontmatter`, `20-the-algorithm`, `30-workflow-routing`, and so on) by a build script,
with a `LATEST` pointer for the current algorithm version. Improve any one component and the whole
system rebuilds, so every later response benefits. His line: a bad result "is almost never because
Claude is dumb, it is because my scaffolding did not provide the right context."
**Original source.** [Personal AI Infrastructure](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/personal-ai-infrastructure).
**Relates to.** Extends your Skills chapter with a maintainability pattern. Most people hand-write one
big CLAUDE.md; the build-script-assembles-context approach is a genuinely under-taught technique.
**Creator's video.** [We're All Building a Single Digital Assistant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUForkn00mk)

### 12. Rate AI by Customization, then Integration, then Competence
**Core insight.** Miessler rates AI harnesses on three axes in a deliberate order: Customization (how
deeply it knows your past, present, and desired states), Integration (can it reach you anywhere, see
what you see, drive any system), and Competence (model, skills, hooks, APIs) last. The reversal is
the point: competence improves fast and is cheap to copy, so durable advantage comes from the
unglamorous customization and integration layers.
**Original source.** [Customization Beats Competence](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/customization-beats-competence). Blog-sourced this run; no confirmed companion video.
**Relates to.** A direct framework for evaluating Claude Code versus other harnesses and for
justifying time spent on hooks, memory, and MCP integrations. Contrarian to the usual "best model
wins" content.

### 13. Reverse-engineer the request: extract the implied wants and the anti-criteria
**Core insight.** Before building, every input gets deconstructed: what was explicitly asked, what was
implied, what the user does NOT want, common failure modes, and gotchas. He calls the failure to do
this "writer's blindness." The anti-criteria (things to avoid) matter as much as the goal criteria and
feed straight into Ideal State.
**Original source.** [Nobody Is Talking About Generalized Hill-Climbing](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/nobody-is-talking-about-generalized-hill-climbing).
**Relates to.** A teachable observe-phase prompt pattern that reduces the "it built the wrong thing
perfectly" failure. Extends your spec-developer material.

### 14. Secure your harness for blast radius, not just probability
**Core insight.** Prompt injection sets your trust ceiling. Miessler runs PAI with layered injection
defenses, filesystem-permission hooks, and (notably) stopped using `--dangerously-skip-permissions`.
He rates current trust around 60% and frames the real defense as impact reduction over probability
reduction: separate low-limit experiment accounts, bounded blast radius, and threat-modeling by
assuming the breach already happened ("a link says 'go read this,' it is an injection, and now my
diary is on LinkedIn"). He deliberately keeps his journal out of the system.
**Original source.** [Personal AI Infrastructure](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/personal-ai-infrastructure) (Security component) and the [Labenz interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvXC7sqso4w).
**Relates to.** Maps directly onto Claude Code permissions and hooks. A practical "secure your agent
harness" video carrying real authority from his security background.
**Creator's video.** [How and Why I Built PAI, with Nathan Labenz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vvXC7sqso4w)

### 15. Hill-climb on your own harness: self-improving scaffolding from sentiment signal
**Core insight.** PAI harvests sentiment signal from every response, overlays it on what was done, and
a "PAIUpdate" skill runs an "UpgradeAlgorithm" workflow that suggests concrete improvements to the
harness itself. The system is "hill-climbing on its own hill-climbing capability," with a dashboard of
parallel Algorithm sessions each tracking their own criteria.
**Original source.** [Nobody Is Talking About Generalized Hill-Climbing](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/nobody-is-talking-about-generalized-hill-climbing).
**Relates to.** An advanced meta-skill demo: a skill that audits and upgrades your other skills from
logged feedback. Rarely shown end to end.

### 16. Fabric: break work into components, encode each as a reusable Pattern, chain them
**Core insight.** Fabric's methodology: capture the desired outcome, break it into components, apply a
focused prompt ("Pattern") to each, expose each as a CLI command, then chain commands into workflows.
The philosophy: AI beats humans at most mental tasks, but humans understand the desired *shape* of the
output, so a human-written Pattern carries that shape.
**Original source.** [The Origin of Fabric](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/fabric-origin-story) and [How My Projects Fit Together](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/how-my-projects-fit-together). Blog-sourced this run.
**Relates to.** The conceptual ancestor of skills and skill-chaining: small, composable,
CLI-invocable, reusable prompt units. A clean contrast or sequel to your Skills and Custom Prompts
videos ("humans own the output shape, AI owns execution").

### 17. AI is the thing that enables the thing (tooling crashes, builders boom)
**Core insight.** Miessler predicts both an AI crash and an AI boom: most "AI tooling" companies get
crushed because tooling commoditizes, while a far bigger boom comes from tiny teams using AI to ship
net-new products, a 1 to 20 person team operating like a 200 to 20,000 person company. AI is a
magnifier of human creativity, not a product category.
**Original source.** [AI Isn't the Thing, It's the Thing That Enables the Thing](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/ai-isn-t-the-thing-it-s-the-thing-that-enables-the-thing). Blog-sourced this run.
**Relates to.** The business motivation for the course: do not build another wrapper, use agentic
coding to ship real products as a solo or small team.

## Positioning and evidence material

Not standalone videos so much as the load-bearing slides and openers for the ideas above.

### 18. The Capability Stack: creativity is only 4% of the job
Knowledge, Understanding, Intelligence, Creativity, Expertise. The "but humans are creative" defense
collapses against McKinsey (2017): only about 4% of US work activities require creativity at the
median human level. Source: [Exactly Why and How AI Will Replace Knowledge Work](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/exactly-why-and-how-ai-will-replace-knowledge-work) (McKinsey Global Institute, "A Future That Works," 2017).

### 19. Human black boxes: Libet, Nader, and the 47% mind-wandering
Myth-busting opener for "humans are special." Libet (1983): the brain initiates a decision about
350ms before conscious awareness. Nader (2000): memory is rewritten on recall. Killingsworth and
Gilbert (2010): people mind-wander 46.9% of waking hours. If your own experts cannot introspect their
reasoning, you extract it by observing behavior and writing it down, which is how you build good
skills. Source: [Exactly Why and How AI Will Replace Knowledge Work](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/exactly-why-and-how-ai-will-replace-knowledge-work).

### 20. AI competes against chaos, not excellence ($438B disengagement)
Companies are not efficient machines AI must beat, they are chaos. Gallup (2025, n=227,347): 21%
engaged, 62% not engaged, 15% actively disengaged, costing $438B in 2024. The deploy bar for an agent
is the messy, undocumented human status quo, not perfection. Source: [Exactly Why and How AI Will Replace Knowledge Work](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/exactly-why-and-how-ai-will-replace-knowledge-work).

### 21. SWE-bench 4% to 72% in a year: the exponential nobody internalizes
Stanford HAI's 2025 AI Index reports SWE-bench solve rates jumping from 4.4% to 71.7% in one year.
This is the canonical agentic-coding benchmark and the cleanest "why learn this now" evidence slide.
Source: [Exactly Why and How AI Will Replace Knowledge Work](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/exactly-why-and-how-ai-will-replace-knowledge-work) (Stanford HAI AI Index 2025).

### 22. Finding the middle: the one-year coding-adoption cadence
Both AI camps overreact; the truth is a fast middle. "We went from 'I'd never let AI code for me' to
'I review what it writes' to 'I don't even write code anymore' in one calendar year," then the
pendulum swung back to "AI can't replace workers" in under six months. A credibility-building course
intro that dodges both hype and doom. Source: [Finding the Middle in AI Narratives](https://danielmiessler.com/blog/finding-the-middle-in-ai-narratives).

### 23. Model-provider trust: sincerity vs proportionality vs capture
From the Dario Amodei debate, mostly AI-policy and biosecurity ethics with one transferable builder
lens: separate (a) is the provider sincere in its safety beliefs, (b) is the safeguard proportional to
the real risk, and (c) does the safeguard double as a competitive moat. These come apart, and the
split is how a builder should weigh gated frontier models versus open weights for their own stack. A
60-second aside, not a module. Source: [Debating the Morality of Dario Amodei](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0FxXr3enprE).
