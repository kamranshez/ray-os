Notes on Bret Victor's closing keynote at UIST/SPLASH, October 2014.

- Representations
	- Responsible for most of the human progress we have made in the last 2000 years.
	- Representation is the "physical form of a thought".
	- Eg, Feynman diagrams.
	- A lot of the "power of an idea" lies in the form in which its expressed as it allows people to think it.
	- When thinking about the great ideas of history, you want to think of the great representations that allowed people to think those ideas.

![[bret-victor-great-representations-slide.png]]

- Dynamic Conversations
	- You're making simulations, programs, etc.
	- Want to have a medium we can model and explore.
	- A future where we can program in seconds with real dynamic working models.
		- (For the video, this can be about audio inputs. A future I imagine is that the models get fast enough that I can have a back-and-forth conversation and it can generate new things on demand on my screen. Or maybe in a 3D world.)
	- 35:40 - He says, you may think that's impossible. How can you program in seconds.
	- (Where I think this is going, you just speak at your screen for a couple hours and the AI is drawing all these interfaces, making decisions, and it's going to the background and being shipped.)
	- (For artifacts, rather than just visualising data, make it easier to model and explore with the model. Eg, rather than just visualising data, can you use previous judgement to give me hypothesis for what would happen if I changed this.)
- Dynamic Reading
	- Just like game designers are surprised by what gamers do in their game, rather than an author sending out something static, readers get something dynamic you can learn from. So eventually authors can be surprised by what their readers are getting out of it. They're getting more out than the author put in.
	- The reader will be able to try out different things.
	- A book should be a space you walk around in.
- Dynamic Library
	- You can walk into a space and get a gist of it.
