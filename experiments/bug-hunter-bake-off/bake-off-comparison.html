Bug-Hunter Bake-Off

Ten independently designed bug-hunting skills, run in parallel against the same codebase, under identical rules, each spawning its own nested subagents. The question is not "what bugs does HyperWhisper have." It is which hunting strategy actually finds real defects when there is no diff to anchor on. The bugs are the measuring instrument, not the product.

Arena: 210 files / ~31k lines TypeScript Model: Opus 5 ~85 nested subagents 2026-07-29

Verdict

★ Best

entry-point-tracer

Enumerate all 88 entry points, trace each hop by hop, inspect the joints between layers rather than the layers.

Reached a class of cross-system seam defect — code versus Stripe, code versus Vercel's deploy ordering — that no other strategy here reached at all. But its counts are unaudited and were never graded on the shared ladder, so the rank is not defensible: see corrections.

Runner-up

hostile-input-hunter

Construct a hostile input, run it, keep only what reproduces.

Every one of its 20 confirmed findings carried an executable repro, the only clean sweep in the field. Wins on proof quality, loses on ceiling: you cannot fuzz your way to an architectural mismatch.

▼ Worst

mutation-survivor-hunter

Mutate the code, run the tests, treat surviving mutants as unpinned behaviour.

Zero confirmed. The thesis presupposes a test suite this codebase lacks where it matters, so a surviving mutant carries no information and the signal collapses. Last on fit, not conduct — and it produced the run's most alarming result, which no scoring axis here can score.

Scorecard

Every hunter was given the same three-rung ladder. The default verdict was refuted, so a finding had to earn its way up:

Refuted  · thrown away

"This looks fragile" with no trigger path. Not counted anywhere. Most candidates ended here, which is the point.

Plausible  · a maybe

Survived scrutiny, but one link in the chain could not be proven. The hunter had to say which link. Counted, but discounted.

Confirmed  · a real bug

A concrete trigger path from a real entry point: named file, named line, named caller, and an input that reaches it.

Separately from the ladder: did the hunter back the finding with something executable (a failing test, a curl, a node one-liner, an observed typecheck error) rather than with an argument? That is the proof rate column, and it is the most informative number in the table. Two hunters can both report 20 confirmed findings while one proved them and the other reasoned about them.

Why proof rate and not confirmed count: across these ten rows, confirmed count correlates with files read at r=+0.82 and with subagent count at r=+0.82, while repro count correlates with neither at significance (+0.47 and +0.31). Both figures come from the same self-reports, so that gap is not a reporting artifact. Fanning out more agents buys more claims, not more proofs — which makes the confirmed column substantially a compute proxy and the proof column the one that resists inflation.

Convergence index — the most load-bearing section

Independent rediscovery is the strongest evidence available that a finding is real. When strategies with nothing in common land on the same file and line, the defect is almost certainly there.

The top item was found four times by four unrelated strategies, and every one of them verified it by running the WHATWG URL parser rather than reasoning about it. Fix these first, regardless of which skill you keep.

What I would actually take from this

Keep three, chosen for coverage rather than rank. entry-point-tracer for cross-system seams, which nothing else here reached; hostile-input-hunter for executable proof, the one axis compute cannot inflate; concurrency-hunter for defects needing two things to happen at once, a class no amount of careful reading finds. They fail in genuinely different directions and their overlap was low, so running all three gets the severe findings, the incontestable ones, and the ones that only appear under load.

Correction to the original pairing argument. This page used to justify the pair by saying their coverage barely overlapped. That is false: 7 of hostile-input-hunter's 20 findings have entry-point-tracer counterparts (#1, #2, #3, #5, #7, #8, #14 against T10-F2, T07-BUG-5, T07-BUG-2/6, T03-BUG-4, T03-BUG-5, T04-#4). The pair is still right, but for a different reason: they differ in evidence class, one producing arguments and the other executable demonstrations, not in which code they visit. Why the winners won takes this apart.

The inverted ladder worked. Verifiers refuted a large share of what finders proposed, including several that read as compelling: a "dead" validator that was live, a Stripe version mismatch that was a typing artifact, a credit bug whose exposure proved bounded and self-correcting. Under diff-review defaults, every one of those ships as a PLAUSIBLE comment.

Coverage honesty was the sleeper axis. The strategies worth trusting are not the ones with the biggest numbers. They are the ones whose refuted lists are longer than their candidate lists.

Caveat on the ranking. Two hunters (marked ※) never got their synthesis pass, because the run was paused. They are scored on what their subagents demonstrably returned, which denies them the step where a hunter deduplicates, re-verifies, and drops its own weak items. That is exactly where spec-gap-hunter initially failed, so it is not a trivial omission.

The middle of the table is close enough that a clean rerun could reorder it. The top is now softer than it first looked: see the corrections below.

Corrections, 2026-07-30. Every row was re-checked against the scorecard block each hunter wrote at the top of its own report. Three rows were wrong when this page was first published.

concurrency-hunter was published as 3 confirmed and is actually 12, moving it from 7th to 3rd. Its own coverage ledger explains why: a transport failure swallowed all six of its lens agents' completion notifications, so it wrote a partial report from its own direct reading, then recovered the agents and published a merged version. The scorecard was taken from the partial one. The 17 in the old files-read column was the count it had read by itself, never its coverage. The direction of that error is the uncomfortable part: the run penalised a hunter for harness flakiness and called it a property of its strategy.

mutation-survivor-hunter was 0/1/6/15/3 and is actually 0/3/7/20/4, which does not move it off last place. git-signal-hunter's proof count was 5, not 6.

The top spot is not defensible and should be read as unranked. entry-point-tracer's ~40 is the largest figure on this page, no independent read of it exists (an attempt to re-extract it from the raw traces failed), and it was assigned by a third party to prose that was never graded on the ladder at all: none of its 13 subagent prompts contain the words REFUTED or PLAUSIBLE, and PLAUSIBLE appears nowhere in its transcripts, which instead self-grade confidence: high. Its number is therefore not commensurable with the nine rows beneath it. Two further claims failed on checking: its redirect-loop lockout was independently found by invariant-hunter's I8 sweep at the same file and line, and the "highest unique-finding count" was never tabulated by anyone. What survives is narrower and still worth keeping the strategy for: entry-point tracing reached a class of cross-system seam defect (Stripe binding by unverified email, Refund-Only revoking via its own webhook, migrations committing before failable build steps) that no other strategy here reached at all.

An earlier correction on this page was itself wrong and is withdrawn. It claimed invariant-hunter had been over-credited with other hunters' candidates. It had not: I1-I8 are its own first eight sweeps, each candidate carrying an explicit ownership tag, and what looked like misattribution was six hunters converging on the same defects. Its real error is the agent count, which is at least 19 (8 sweeps + 6 sweeps + 5 verifiers), not 14.

Ranks 5 to 10 are a race outcome, not a strategy property. All ten hunters drew on one shared 20-slot subagent pool and the four largest fan-outs alone demanded about 55. oracle-hunter lost its entire verification wave ("That phase did not run"), subsystem-auditor lost Angle D and all but one verifier, spec-gap-hunter received zero subagent output, and mutation-survivor-hunter had 3 spawns rejected outright. The bottom half of this table is partly a record of who lost a resource race, and should be read as one-directionally understated.
