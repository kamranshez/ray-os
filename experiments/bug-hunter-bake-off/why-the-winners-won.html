Why the winners won

Ten bug-hunting strategies, one codebase, ~85 agents. This page works backwards from the defects that were actually confirmed to the property that surfaced them — and finds that most of the winners did not win for the reason their own instructions claim.

Analysis: 3 independent Fable agents Lenses: mechanism · confounds · generality 2026-07-30 ← the scoreboard
The short answer

The ranking is explained almost entirely by two variables, and neither of them is a thesis: whether the strategy enumerated a finite surface and swept all of it rather than sampling a region, and whether it could execute something rather than argue.

Thesis content mattered mostly for what it forbade the hunter from looking at. The single most productive mechanism in the entire run is one that no skill claims and no skill was built for.

01Enumerate and sweep, or sample and hope

Sort the ten by how much of the 210-file arena they touched, and the table sorts itself into two groups with almost nothing in between.

Figure 1 — coverage splits the field before any thesis does
The gap is the story. Every strategy above the line defines a finite set up front — 88 entry points, every input surface, one invariant × every file — and then accounts for each member. Every strategy below it picks a region and reads hard. There is no middle. The four sweepers took the top four places on confirmed count and the four samplers took the bottom four, and this happened across four completely unrelated theses on each side.

02But coverage is bought with compute, and compute buys claims, not proofs

Which raises the obvious objection: maybe the winners just spent more. Mostly yes — on one axis, and revealingly not on the other.

Figure 2 — the same self-reports, two very different relationships
Confirmed findings track compute at r=+0.82. Executable repros do not track it at all (+0.31, below the p<.05 threshold of 0.632 for n=10). Both numbers come out of the same self-reported scorecards, so the gap cannot be a reporting artifact. Fanning out more agents buys more claims; it does not buy more proofs. That makes proof rate the only volume metric in the scoreboard that resists inflation — and it is why hostile-input-hunter's clean sweep of 20 repros on 20 findings is the most robust single result in the run.

The exception that teaches more than the rule

concurrency-hunter reached ~150 files on 6 subagents, matching the 15-agent entrant's coverage at 40% of the spend. Its ledger shows how: one broad mapping agent opened 120 files, then five narrow lenses swept what the mapper found. Correcting its row is what dropped the files~subagents correlation from +0.87 to +0.65.

So coverage is not simply purchased. Fan-out shape beats fan-out count: one wide cheap mapper plus narrow deep lenses dominates many independent deep divers, because the divers each re-pay the discovery cost. This is the most directly actionable finding on this page.

03What actually generated the findings

Ten distinct acts of attention appear across the run. Ranked by yield, the leader is not anybody's thesis — it is a move four different hunters stumbled into while nominally doing something else, each giving it a different name.

The buried winner

M2 — read two siblings that do one job, find the guard present in one and absent in the other, then find the comment that says the missing guard matters.

This produced every single convergent finding in the run. Four hunters rediscovered it independently and none of them was built for it: git-signal called it adjacency, oracle called it a guard-asymmetry scan, concurrency called it constraint-backing, subsystem-auditor called it contract auditing. It is cheap, it needs no infrastructure, and its verdicts are near-unarguable because the codebase convicts itself: the sibling's comment usually states the exact harm.

The canonical instance: /usage caches any non-2xx as "license invalid" for an hour, while its sibling auth.ts:62-73 has the exact guard and a comment naming the exact harm. Six hunters found it. One transient 502 during a redeploy locks a paying customer out for an hour across all 17 machines, and it cannot self-heal because the next read is a cache hit.

The winner's number does not survive counting

entry-point-tracer's ~40 confirmed is a post-hoc promotion of a subset of ~122 candidate blocks across its 13 traces (T03=9, T04=12, T05=13, T06=8, T07=8, T08=11, T09=8, T10=10, T11=10, T12=6, T13=7, T14=13, T15=7). Each carries only a self-rated confidence field. None passed through its own verify or prove phase — and none of its 13 subagent prompts contains the words REFUTED, PLAUSIBLE, or "default verdict". The shared ladder was never propagated to the winner's evidence base, so its count is not commensurable with the nine rows beneath it.

At least two of its candidates were independently refuted elsewhere in the same run, and one of its two headline findings — the redirect-loop lockout — was independently rediscovered by invariant-hunter's I8 sweep at the same file and line, naming the same trigger. The claim "neither was reached by any other hunter" is false.

What survives is narrower and still worth keeping: entry-point tracing reached a class of cross-system seam defect that nothing else here reached at all. That claim needs no count to stand.

05Where the bugs actually were

A strategy can only win by pointing at where the defects are. So: where were they? Not where the codebase's shape suggests.

Figure 3 — one balance, four owners, no ownership rule
The defect-generating mechanism. Money state lives in four places. Postgres was hardened — FOR UPDATE, unique indexes on four columns, several carrying comments naming the race they close. The other three were never given an ownership rule. Every expensive defect in this run is a disagreement between two of these four stores, and every survivor sits either upstream of Postgres (a gate reading a cache) or on a dedupe key chosen so an existing constraint can never fire (sourceId: crypto.randomUUID()). Two structural amplifiers: 17 always-on Fly machines, so no in-process guarantee is ever global; and serverless on the other side, whose redeploys generate exactly the transient 502 that poisons the cache.

The hypothesis that lost

"Many near-duplicate provider adapters, so sibling-diffing wins." Close to refuted. divergence-hunter exists to diff duplicates and scored 2. The obvious duplicate family (account/* against license/*) turns out to be 9-12 line wrappers over real implementations — deliberately factored, nothing to diverge. And only 1 of invariant-hunter's 6 sweeps was a clean whole-class omission harvest.

The hypothesis that won

"Money-state transitions under concurrency and partial failure." 11 of concurrency-hunter's 12 confirmed findings are money-state. 4 of 7 convergence-index items are. And mutation-survivor-hunter's 7 surviving mutants land on the same ~300 lines — three unrelated methods pointing at one place. That is a mechanism, not a theme, which is what makes it a real cluster.

Why the distinction pays: "integration seams" predicts the winner correctly for the wrong reason and tells you to diff all boundaries. "Money-state under concurrency and partial failure" tells you to trace state ownership across cache, retry and lifecycle boundaries — a far smaller target, and the one to aim a rerun at.

A second cluster, smaller and more severe — and nothing was aimed at it

Auth and session state-transition guards: the login CSRF, the cookie-presence versus session-validity lockout, the sign-out route missing a return after revoking the session. About 10 findings, but they hold the two most severe in the run. Money-state is the volume cluster; auth-state is the severity cluster. No single strategy was pointed at both — which is the real argument for running a small portfolio rather than picking a winner.

06How much of this is about HyperWhisper?

Most of it. Four of the ten were scored on a codebase they were not looking at, each precondition verified by direct measurement. Pick a different codebase and the table reorders violently.

07So what actually leads to winning

1
Enumerate a finite surface and account for every member. Not because enumeration finds bugs, but because it makes coverage checkable — and the characteristic failure of whole-codebase hunting is a report that reads exhaustive when it was a sample. This is the one property worth carrying to every codebase, even where the strategy that owns it would finish 8th.
2
Be able to run something. Execution is the only axis in this run that compute could not inflate, and it collapses verdict ambiguity: the demonstration is the verdict, so the method produces almost no maybes. It is also the best refuter — the same harness that proved 20 findings killed a 25-row catalog of candidate number bugs.
3
Cross the process boundary. Read node_modules, the platform's deploy ordering, the third party's real delivery semantics. Highest severity per finding of any mechanism, and only two of ten hunters were permitted to do it. Both headline severities came from here.
4
Diff siblings and let the codebase convict itself. The highest-yield mechanism in the run and nobody's thesis. Promote it to a first-class hunter and you capture most of the yield at a fraction of the cost.
5
One wide cheap mapper, then narrow deep lenses. Shape, not count. Six well-shaped agents matched fifteen poorly-shaped ones on coverage.
6
Check your preconditions before you start. Four of ten entrants were scored on codebases they were not looking at. The transferable result here is not "use entry-point-tracer" — it is the selection rule: identify the codebase's defect-generating mechanism, then check each strategy's precondition against it.
The cheap way to test all of this

Run the same ten against a mature library with a dense test suite. If mutation-survivor and hostile-input top the table and entry-point-tracer falls out of the top five, the selection rule is real and this run's ranking is a fact about HyperWhisper. If entry-point-tracer wins again, this whole analysis is wrong and the ranking is closer to a general result than any of it claims.

08What this page is not sure about

  • Convergence measures noticing, not verdict. One row of the convergence index was noticed by three hunters and then independently refuted by two. Counting noticings had it exactly backwards, and that row is now withdrawn.
  • Convergence also has a selection bias. The findings that converge are by construction the ones many strategies can reach, so the index partly measures how cheap a finding was. The six-way item sits in two functions about twenty lines apart in one file. Unique findings are the axis that discriminates between strategies, and it is the axis this run measured worst.
  • Ranks 5-10 are partly a resource race. All ten hunters drew on one shared 20-slot subagent pool while the four biggest fan-outs alone wanted ~55. oracle lost its entire verification wave, spec-gap received zero subagent output, divergence lost three of five subagents and never ran the sweep that owns its best finding, mutation-survivor had three spawns rejected. Read the bottom half as understated in one direction.
  • The defect taxonomy counts are ±30%. The classification agents were lost to a network drop and only proportions were recoverable. Category shares are reliable; individual counts are estimates.
  • n=1 codebase, n=1 run, self-graded. Every number here is a hunter's own assessment of its own work, and three scorecard rows were transcribed wrong on first publication. A five-line parser generating the scoreboard from the reports would have caught all three.
